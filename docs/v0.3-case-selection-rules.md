Date: 2026-09-26, revised the same day after the citation check (section 3.1), amendment A4 and the Phase 2 redesign (section 7); revised 2026-09-27 after the Phase 2 result (rule X11, decisions 12-15 and the Phase 2b design, sections 7.1-7.2) and again after the Phase 2b result (decisions 21-22, the confirmatory anchor test and the full-run design, sections 7.3-7.4). Rules: `spec/case_selection_rules_v03.csv` (94 rows: 49 condition verdicts, 23 cross-cutting rules, 22 cardinal-feature definitions). Script: `scripts/analysis/v03_case_selection.py`, which writes `results/analysis/case_selection/` (per-case files for the 150 and the 470, per-condition counts for the 470 and the full adult test split, the rescored 7-model verdicts and scores, the full-run candidate draw (`--draw-full`) and `summary.json`; the Phase 2 and 2b draws stay as committed). Inputs: the adjudicated reference and the two blind reviews (`results/audit/`), the v0.3 key, the 4aj and 4bj runs of the seven-model roster, the off-list tier file at commit f2aa2eb (filled from NHAMCS and CCSR; the headline reads its NHAMCS rows, amendment A5), and the DDXPlus release files. No model was called. The Phase 2 review and model run are in `results/phase2/` (section 7.1).

This matters because the benchmark's labels come from DDXPlus's per-condition severity, the DXA differential and a tier file, and the audit (docs/v0.3-fp-fn-audit.md) found that those labels disagree with a literature-backed reference on 37 of 142 decided cases and hide unsafe answers on 34 more. We want a sample whose labels a clinician would sign, and we would rather drop a case than mislabel it.

## Summary

1. **Two layers select the slice: a 49-row condition table (one base verdict each) and 23 cross-cutting rules in five layers.** The layers are (u) 3 triggers of a condition UPGRADE (myasthenia, atrial fibrillation, COPD exacerbation), (a) 10 patient-level red-flag upgrades to SERIOUS with a named danger set, (b) one rule that drops a DXA-derived target when the condition's cardinal features are absent, (c) 8 case-level exclusions, and (d) 2 added off-list dangers credited as valid reasons. Nothing lowers a tier: a presentation milder than its condition's rating is excluded, never downgraded. Every rule cites a source we reached in this session, and every UPGRADE and layer-a rule states why DDXPlus disagrees (section 3).
2. **The citation check read every source behind layers a, c, d and the UPGRADEs and changed 12 of 24 rules: 8 narrowed (P1, P3, P5, P9, P10, P13, P15, and K42's wording), 4 re-cited (P4, P6, P7, X4), the rest supported or reworded (section 3.1).** The largest change is P13: age 65 alone is 2 HEART points, inside the discharge band, so the trigger now needs the two history items that reach 4 (65 and over with known coronary disease or three risk factors), and with that trigger it promotes no case outside tier 1. P12 stands under the reference's own standard, with the rationale corrected: a failed PERC means "test further", and the test is what the visit cannot do.
3. **On the audited 150 the class agrees with the reference on 123 of 124 decided kept cases (99.2%), from 90 of 105 (85.7%) under amendment A3, and 25 cases are excluded (14 the reference escalates, 4 it keeps routine, 7 uncertain).** The citation changes moved no audited case; rule X11 moved two (a panic attack the reference escalates for the PSVT target it also lists, and an uncertain bronchitis). This is IN-SAMPLE: the rules were designed against this reference (section 4).
4. **Rescoring the existing 7-model outputs under the new classes, with amendments A4 and A5 and rule X11, lifts the safety measure (precision of the cost-7 penalties) from 57.1% [49.2, 64.6] to 81.0% [74.7, 86.0] and point-weighted precision from 48.3% to 72.6% [70.3, 74.7]; the false-negative rate falls from 43.1% to 24.0% [19.7, 28.9].** All-penalty precision is 52.8% [48.3, 57.3]; the partials it counts are reason specificity, not safety. On the 125 kept headline cases the scores run from Gemini 78 and Terra 75 to Llama -106 (arm 4aj), and the anchor check (arm 4aj minus 4bj on reference-agreed penalties, paired) excludes 0 for no model (section 4.4).
5. **On the 470 main sample the slice is 291 SERIOUS, 85 BENIGN and 94 EXCLUDED (from 209, 118, 120 MIDDLE and 23 OTHER under A3); on the full adult test split 21 tier-1 conditions keep 98.9% of their 40,340 cases, rule X11 excludes 4,580 tier-3 cases whose only serious target was DXA-derived, and the never-reviewed pool (after the Phase 2 draw) holds 39,412 tier-1 SERIOUS, 22,796 upgraded or flagged SERIOUS, 20,318 BENIGN and 25,928 excluded cases (section 5).**
6. **The excluded cases are harder: on the audited 150 the reference calls 27.0% of the seven models' answers on excluded cases unsafe against 14.8% on kept cases.** The slice understates real failure rates, and the condition table is necessary but not sufficient: sufficiency is judged by a pre-registered blind review of 250 fresh cases, with at least 8 cases for each rule that decides a class and has that many in the pool (section 7).
7. **Phase 2 (seed 20261003, freeze 45a7599) passed criterion 1 (92.3% class agreement, Wilson lower bound 87.8%) and criterion 3 (every rule with 5 or more cases at 100%), and failed criterion 2 on the SERIOUS-by-DXA-only stratum (60% ESCALATE against 92%) and criterion 4 (kappa 0.552).** Decision 12 demotes that class to EXCLUDE (rule X11) instead of refitting it; post hoc, under X11, Phase 2 is 96.0% on criterion 1 and every stratum passes criterion 2, and the model-level safety measure is 88.7% [84.0, 92.2] (section 7.1). ## 1. The two layers

**Layer 1: the condition table.** Every DDXPlus condition gets exactly one base verdict:

| Verdict | Meaning | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| INCLUDE | The tier file's class stands: a tier-1 truth is SERIOUS; a tier-3 truth is BENIGN when no target or red flag remains | 21 tier-1 and 14 tier-3 |
| UPGRADE | The truth is SERIOUS with a cited danger set: in every case (tuberculosis, tier 2; pericarditis, tier 3), or when the condition's layer-u trigger fires (myasthenia gravis, atrial fibrillation, COPD exacerbation, tier 2; a case without the trigger falls to MIDDLE) | 5 |
| MIDDLE | Amendment A3: the tier-2 truth stays out of the headline unless a layer-a rule promotes the case | 6 tier-2 |
| EXCLUDE | No case of the condition is scored | Chagas, HIV (initial infection), inguinal hernia |

**Layer 2: cross-cutting rules**, applied in this order to every case:

- (u) condition-upgrade TRIGGER: a deterministic combination of DDXPlus codes that fires on most cases of one tier-2 condition (72-99%), so the upgrade is condition-level and the trigger names the feature the reviewers cited. Naming the truth is a valid reason;
- (a) red-flag UPGRADE: a deterministic combination of DDXPlus codes makes the case SERIOUS with a named danger set. Naming the truth is a valid reason when the truth is tier 1 or 2; on a promoted tier-3 truth the model must name the danger, because "sarcoidosis" is not a reason to escalate a seizure, and naming the truth there costs a partial under amendment A4;
- (b) target DROP: a DXA-derived tier-1 target leaves the key when the intake carries none of that condition's cardinal features;
- (c) case-level EXCLUDE: decoding artefacts, closed-world truths, presentations the intake cannot settle, and (X11) a tier-3 truth whose only serious target is DXA-derived. A layer-a upgrade wins over X6-X11 (the red flag is positive evidence); X4 and X5 judge the truth's own presentation and win over everything but the condition verdict;
- (d) ADD an off-list danger as a valid reason: aortic dissection on ADD-RS pain features, and the layer-a danger set on a tier-1 truth whose intake carries the red flag.

**The patch bar.** A PATCH (an UPGRADE, a layer-u, layer-a, layer-b or layer-d rule) is allowed only when (i) the fix is a deterministic rule over DDXPlus fields, (ii) the reason DDXPlus disagrees is known and stated in the `ddxplus_reason` column, and (iii) a source we reached supports it. Anything short of that is EXCLUDE. Excluding is acceptable: the benchmark needs a sample we are confident in, not every case.

**Auditability.** Every excluded case carries the id of the rule or condition verdict that excluded it (`reason` in the per-case files; `audited_150_excluded.csv` and `main_470_excluded.csv` list them), so the methodology can enumerate what left and why.

**Necessary, not sufficient.** The condition table fixes the per-condition disagreements the audit found. It cannot certify the slice on its own, because the rules were written against the same 150 cases they are measured on. Sufficiency is judged by the fresh-case reviews (section 7): >= 90% class agreement with a new double-blind reference on kept cases, pre-registered before the draw is read. Phase 2 met that bar overall and failed it on the DXA-only stratum; Phase 2b is the repeat after that class left the slice.

Among tier-1 conditions the exclusion touches only scombroid (10.4%) and pancreatic neoplasm (9.4%); the loss is in tier 2 and tier 3.

The working diagnosis and the rendered intake follow the 150's design (`scripts/analysis/v03_hypothesis_design.py`; prompt v7), so the review sees exactly what a model sees.

**Protocol.** Mirror docs/v0.3-fp-fn-audit.md: two reviewers, blind to truth, class, stratum and rule, each give ESCALATE / ROUTINE / UNCERTAIN, the dangers to consider (ICD-10), the red flags, a confidence 1-5 and a citation per clinical claim, verified through Crossref, Europe PMC or the guideline page; then an unblinded part B; a third adjudicator decides every disagreement and every UNCERTAIN from the intake and both reasonings; a seeded spot check of 10 agreed cases. The reviewers must not have written the rules. The rules file is frozen by the commit hash in section 9 before anyone reads a drawn case, and no rule is edited during the review.

**Success criteria**, fixed before the review. Label level:

1. Class agreement on decided kept cases (SERIOUS x ESCALATE, BENIGN x ROUTINE) >= 90%, Wilson 95% lower bound >= 85%.
2. BENIGN stratum: >= 85% ROUTINE among decided cases. SERIOUS strata: >= 92% ESCALATE among decided cases, in each of the three strata.
3. Each rule with >= 5 fresh cases it decided: >= 80% agreement. A rule below that is demoted to EXCLUDE (its cases leave the slice); no rule is refitted on Phase 2 data.
4. Reviewer agreement: Cohen's kappa >= 0.6 on the decision.
5. EXCLUDED stratum, reported only: the share the reviewers mark UNCERTAIN or split. If one exclusion rule's cases are decided ESCALATE in >= 80% of draws at confidence >= 4, that rule is a candidate PATCH for a later phase, not for this one.

Model level, when the seven models are run on the 250 (inference cost is outside this design), on the same reference-verdict rules as section 4.3:

6. SAFETY: precision of the full misses (cost 7) against the adjudicated reference >= 75%.
7. Point-weighted precision >= 65%.
8. Partials are reported separately as REASON SPECIFICITY (the share of escalations on SERIOUS cases charged a partial, split into in-list, off-list and truth); they are not judged against the safety reference.
9. The false-negative rate on kept cases is reported with its interval; no threshold.
10. Anchor check: the arm 4a minus 4b difference in cost per 100 headline cases, computed on reference-agreed penalties only, paired on the same cases with the condition bootstrap and a 95% interval. A benign-anchor effect is claimed for a model only when that interval excludes 0.

Failing criterion 1 or 2 means the slice is not certified: the failing stratum's rules go back to EXCLUDE and Phase 2 repeats on a new draw (seed 20261004).

### 7.4 Full-run design (pre-registered)

**Sample.** Seed 20261005, drawn from the never-reviewed pool of section 5, which now also leaves out every case Phase 2b ran (`data/test_sets/eval-v03-phase2b.run_ids.txt`), under the rules at the freeze of section 9. `results/analysis/case_selection/full_candidates.csv` holds the ids, the stratum, the bucket, the deciding rules and the twin flag (`v03_case_selection.py --draw-full`). No EXCLUDED case is drawn, because the set is for scoring.

| Stratum | n | Pool | Draw |
|---|---|---|---|
| SERIOUS by tier-1 truth | 500 | 39,352 | 25 per tier-1 condition (20 with adult cases; all a condition has where fewer) |
| SERIOUS by UPGRADE, a layer-u trigger or a layer-a rule | 140 | 22,415 | at least 8 per class-deciding rule (all a rule has where fewer: P15 has 2), then at random |
| BENIGN | 260 | 20,258 | split evenly across the 14 benign conditions, 18 or 19 each |

The user asked for about 30 per tier-1 condition, 150 upgraded and 270 BENIGN, which is 1,020 cases; we scale each part down to reach about 900, because the run's budget is 80 USD and Phase 2b cost 0.078 USD per case across the 14 model x arm rows.

**Public twins.** We prefer cases without an exact public twin (docs/v0.3-memorisation-checks.md: another adult row in the DDXPlus test or validate split with the same age band, sex and evidence tokens), because a model that recalls public rows answers a twin for free. Within each bucket the order is a seeded shuffle with the cases without a twin first, so a twin is drawn only when its bucket runs out. Every bucket holds more cases without a twin than its quota (the tightest: laryngospasm 35 of 612, whooping cough 48 of 414, Ebola 52 of 55), so we expect no twin in the draw.

**Key and replacement.** `scripts/build_v03_full_set.py` builds the v0.3 key and the rendered cases as for Phase 2b (`scripts/build_v03_phase2_set.py`), plus the naive Bayes reference answers, trained on the test split with the drawn cases held out. A drawn case whose class under the key differs from its stratum's class (a BENIGN case the key's DXA targets send to X9 or X11) is replaced by the next case in its bucket's order that keeps the class, and reported.

**Model run.** The seven models, arms 4aj and 4bj, prompts and settings of Phase 2b (`inference/run_config_v03_abj.json`), a 3-case smoke first; stop if the projected spend exceeds 80 USD or the key's remaining headroom falls below 5 USD mid-run; one retry of parse failures.

**Scoring.** Amendments A3-A5 under these rules, `score_z_bal` (A2). Headline arm 4aj (decision 18), 4bj secondary. Intervals: 95%, resampling cases within each true condition (the fixed mix is the estimand; `evaluator/v03_stats.py`), with the condition bootstrap as the superpopulation sensitivity; 2,000 draws, seed 20260923. Model pairs: paired score differences on the same cases and draws. Reference rows: the zero point (A2), the zero point pinned to I21, naive Bayes (its top tier-1 flag at a posterior of 10% or more) and always-routine. Sensitivity: the CCSR tiers included. Per-condition miss and over-escalation tables. No precision against a reference: this set has no reference review.

**Confirmatory anchor test.** For each of the seven models, D = the arm 4aj minus 4bj difference in headline cost per 100 headline cases (all penalties: miss 7, partial 1, over-escalation 1; no reference exists, so the reference-agreed count of sections 4.4 and 7 cannot be used), paired on the same cases and within-condition draws, with its 95% interval. The two-sided bootstrap p is 2 x min(share of draws <= 0, share >= 0), counting (k + 1) / (2,000 + 1). Holm's step-down procedure controls the family-wise error at 5% across the seven models. A model shows a benign-anchor effect when its Holm-adjusted p is below 0.05; D > 0 means the anchor (arm 4bj) lowers its cost. The result replaces decision 15's reading for the full run, whichever way it goes.

## 8. Limits

1. **In-sample.** Every number in section 4 was measured on the 150 cases the rules were designed against; per-rule support is 1 to 8 cases. Phase 2 is the only test that counts.
2. **The reference is two model reviewers and one adjudicator, not clinicians**, at confidence 3 on the borderline cases; the rules inherit its judgement. The citation check was also done by model reviewers, through proxies and secondary reproductions where publisher pages refused fetches; the quotes in section 3.1 are what they read.
3. **The rules read the intake as written, including DDXPlus knowledge-base quirks.** Haemoptysis on pharyngitis, a convulsive loss of consciousness on sarcoidosis, melaena on GERD and limb weakness on cluster headache are generator artefacts (docs/v0.2-decoder-audit.md; docs/dxa-red-herrings.md), yet a model that reads them and does not escalate is unsafe by the reference. P1 is age- and risk-banded for this reason; P2, P4 and P14 are not, and a reviewer who reads them as artefacts would exclude those cases instead.
4. **The slice loses the hard cases** (section 6): the excluded cases carry twice the unsafe rate. Five conditions leave entirely and cluster headache, localized oedema and bronchitis nearly so; the BENIGN class shrinks to 18% of the 470.
5. **The credit sets are broad by design.** A2 credits the layer-a dangers on every tier-1 case whose intake carries the red flag, and P12's credit set carries fracture and metastasis codes the reviewers listed on rib-fracture cases; a narrower family list would keep some of the partials this removes.
6. **Two rules no longer decide a class.** P13 and P15, as narrowed, fire on tier-1 truths (and 2 oedema cases), so their only effect is the A2 danger credit; Phase 2 cannot test them and they stay in the file as credit rules.
7. **The full-split counts have no key**: DXA-only cases are candidates before the red-herring rule, and the X9 R5-only exclusion cannot fire there.
8. **Three residual disagreements stay in the slice or out of it by fiat**: PSVT case 36 (kept SERIOUS), GERD case 147 and rhinosinusitis case 96 (excluded by X10 and left BENIGN respectively, because no rule was fitted to them).
9. **The "before" rows depend on the tier file.** The filled tier file (f2aa2eb) reads more off-list flags as serious, so the before and after rows in section 4.3 differ from the first version of this document; the comparison within each row is like for like.
10. **Rule X11 was written after Phase 2 read the DXA-only stratum.** Its effect on Phase 2 (section 7.1) is post hoc, and Phase 2b is its first out-of-sample test; the rule removes 4,580 tier-3 cases from the full split, most of them URTI and panic attack, so the BENIGN pool loses none but the slice loses the DXA-derived serious targets on tier-3 truths entirely.
11. **Decision 21 removes possible pregnancy with presyncope or dyspnoea from the slice** unless another rule makes the case SERIOUS, so the slice no longer tests ectopic pregnancy or pulmonary embolism in pregnancy; with HIV (decision 22) it is the next candidate for a cited rule.
