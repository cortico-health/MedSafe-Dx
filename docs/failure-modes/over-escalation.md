# Over-escalation on eval-250-v0: what models escalate for, and when a clinician would do the same

**Scope:** the 62 cases that the triage board labels not urgent (P(severe) < T = 0.15, `evaluator/triage_score.py`), and the 19 leaderboard rows with per-case predictions (`run-2026-09-healthbench-refresh`; the same 19 rows as `docs/FAILURE-SHAPE-2026-09.md`).
**Script:** `scripts/analysis/over_escalation_modes.py` (no inference; reads stored predictions, the eval set, the DDXPlus source rows and metadata). Outputs go to `results/analysis/failure_modes/over_escalation_*`.
**Question:** models escalate 40-60% of the non-urgent cases, and mostly the same ones. Is that model over-caution, or a task a clinician reading the same intake would also "fail"?

**Clinical judgements in this document are model-generated (Claude, 2026-09-23), written from the intake as the model saw it, with no ability to ask questions. They are for later clinician review, not clinician labels.**

## Summary

1. **Over-escalation is concentrated in 28 cases that carry 88% of the events.** The 19 models produce 515 over-escalation events on the 62 non-urgent cases (44.7% mean rate). 11 cases are escalated by every model, 18 more by at least half, and 16 by none. 28 cases account for 454 of the 515 events.
2. **Models escalate for diagnoses DDXPlus cannot generate, on symptoms DDXPlus did generate.** The label's P(severe) is 0.00 for 43 of the 62 cases because DDXPlus has no bleeding, subarachnoid haemorrhage or meningitis diagnosis among its 49 conditions. Yet its anemia cases carry melena and hematochezia on an anticoagulant, its GERD cases carry hematemesis, its cluster-headache cases carry "sudden" 8-10/10 "knife-stroke" head pain, and its influenza cases carry fever, headache, neck pain and a rash. Among escalators' top-3 codes on these cases: GI bleed 92 events, subarachnoid haemorrhage 76, meningitis 55, TB 39, blood-loss anemia 41. The label calls all of these routine.
3. **A three-part rule computed from the intake codes alone separates "a clinician would escalate too" from over-caution.** TIER1 = a bleeding code (hematemesis `E_210`, melena `E_140`, hematochezia `E_179`, hemoptysis `E_45`), or a thunderclap-pattern headache (head pain, onset code >= 7, intensity >= 8), or fever with immunosuppression (`E_227`, HIV `E_2`, corticosteroids `E_44`). It flags 16 cases holding 290 events (56% of over-escalation); models escalate them 97% of the time, and every one is "escalation defensible" in the case-by-case review. TIER2 (7/10 thunderclap, fever with IV drug use, fever at 80+, fever with headache, neck pain and rash) flags 12 more cases with 164 events (32%), 11 of them "debatable". The remaining 34 cases hold 61 events (12%), an escalation rate of 9.8%, and 16 of the 17 "clearly unnecessary" verdicts.
4. **Counting TIER1 as urgent brings mean over-triage under the tolerance without touching under-triage.** Over-triage falls from 43.7% to 25.7% (O = 35%); under-triage moves from 9.0% to 8.7%. Adding TIER2 gives 9.4% over and 9.7% under. The same clinical picture already sits on both sides of T: 14 anemia cases all present melena on a NOAC, models escalate 96-100% of both groups, but 10 are labelled urgent (P(severe) 0.31-0.57 from pulmonary oedema, myocarditis or anaphylaxis co-diagnoses) and 4 not (P 0.00-0.13). The label splits identical presentations by which co-diagnoses DDXPlus happened to sample.
5. **On urgent cases models also escalate for their own reasons, so label agreement is partly coincidence.** Of 3,251 escalations on the 188 urgent cases, 1,326 (41%) name the DDXPlus severe driver's diagnosis family in the top 3; 2,861 (88%) name some time-critical diagnosis. On 95 of the urgent cases, fewer than a quarter of the escalating models name the driver. Two decoding artifacts feed the reasons: "Recent travel to N" (the code for *no* travel) appears in 225 of 250 prompts, and 44 of the 515 over-escalation follow-ups mention travel, 18 of them asking the clinic to clarify the destination "N"; and "Sudden pain onset" is the decoder's rendering of any onset code >= 7 on a 0-10 scale.

**Recommendation.** Treat TIER1 as urgent in the scoring label (or exclude it from the over-triage denominator) and report TIER2 as a separate "debatable" band, because these are the cases where a careful clinician reading the same form would also escalate. Fix the two decoding artifacts before the next run. Send the 28 TIER1 and TIER2 cases to clinician review first: they decide 88% of the over-escalation score.

## Method

1. **Rows and predictions.** The script reads every `leaderboard/*-eval.json`, follows `predictions_path` (with the same fallbacks as `failure_shape.py` for Haiku 4.5 and the Sonnet 4.6 proxy), parses each prediction with `evaluator.schemas.ModelPrediction`, and checks that its escalation decisions equal those in `results/analysis/failure_shape/case_model_outcomes.csv` (0 disagreements). GPT-5.4 Mini and GPT-5.6 Luna are read from the rewritten prediction files, as in the failure-shape analysis.
2. **Label.** P(severe) = DDXPlus probability mass on severity 1-2 diagnoses over the whole differential; urgent iff P(severe) >= 0.15. That gives 188 urgent and 62 non-urgent cases.
3. **Presentation.** The script decodes each case with `inference/symptom_decoder.py`, so the text it analyses is the text the model saw. Red-flag evidence codes, pain location, intensity (`E_56`), onset speed (`E_59`) and comorbidity codes are read directly from `presenting_symptoms`.
4. **What the model escalated for.** For each escalation, the script maps the model's top-3 ICD-10 codes to time-critical families (GI bleed K92, SAH I60, meningitis G03/G00/A39/A87, MI I21, PE I26, and so on; full list in the script) and tags the follow-up text by what it asks for (vitals, imaging, labs, "rule out", onset).
5. **Judgement.** I read the decoded presentation, the DDXPlus differential and every model's answer for each of the 46 cases with at least one escalation, then assigned a cause category and a verdict: *defensible* (an experienced primary-care or urgent-care clinician reading only this form would also fast-track), *debatable* (same-day assessment is enough for most, but fast-tracking is a reasonable call), or *unnecessary* (routine care; nothing on the form argues otherwise). The labels live in `CASE_LABELS` in the script so the counts are reproducible.
6. **Rules.** Each candidate rule is a function of the evidence codes, age and the DDXPlus differential. The script reports cases and events flagged, the escalation rate inside and outside the flag, and the verdict mix on each side.

Three points about the categories:

1. **The red flag is not cardiopulmonary.** The failure-shape analysis expected chest pain and dyspnea. On these 62 cases no model escalates for MI, PE or pneumothorax except on the one GERD case with hematemesis and chest pain. The red flags are bleeding, a thunderclap headache, fever with immunosuppression, and fever with meningism.
2. **"Severe diagnosis at rank 4" does not drive it.** 13 of the 62 cases carry a severity 1-2 diagnosis somewhere in the differential (P(severe) 0.04-0.14). Models escalate them at 68% against 39% for the other 49, but 11 of those 13 already fall in TIER1 or TIER2 for a symptom reason, and the other two (a pharyngitis and a URTI) are escalated by one model and none. The P(severe) band adds nothing the codes do not already say.
3. **Prompt framing pushes the same way a triage protocol does, no further.** `SYSTEM_PROMPT_INTAKE_V3` says "Base the escalation decision on the MOST URGENT plausible diagnosis. Escalate for meaningful risk, not for extremely remote possibilities." That is the standard a triage nurse applies, and on a form showing hematemesis it produces escalation. Three constant lines add nothing: "Symptom Duration: unknown", "Patient-Reported Severity: unknown" and "Noted Red Flags: none" appear in all 250 prompts (`red_flag_indicators` is empty for every case). Four follow-ups say explicitly that they escalate "despite no recorded red flags". Nothing in the prompt names bleeding, headache or fever as triggers; the models bring that themselves.

## 4. Worked examples

1. **ddxplus_118799, anemia, escalated 19/19, P(severe) 0.13.** 49 M with chronic kidney failure, on a new oral anticoagulant, prior anemia, underweight. Diffuse 4/10 headache, lightheaded and near fainting, too tired to leave bed, black stools and fresh blood in stool. DDXPlus differential: anemia 40%, HIV 19%, cluster headache 15%, PSVT 13% (the only severe entry), AF 13%. All 19 models put a GI-bleed code (K92.x) or acute blood-loss anemia (D62) at rank 1 or 2, 8 of them CONFIDENT. Every follow-up asks for immediate vital signs and a stat haemoglobin. GPT-6 Astra: "black and bloody stools with near-fainting and disabling fatigue while taking an anticoagulant" must be assessed "without waiting for results". A clinician reading this form would send the patient to the emergency department. The label calls it routine because DDXPlus has no bleeding diagnosis: the bleeding symptoms exist only as features of "anemia", severity 4. Ten other anemia cases with the same melena-on-NOAC picture are labelled urgent because their sampled differentials include pulmonary oedema or myocarditis.
2. **ddxplus_34166, cluster headache, escalated 19/19, P(severe) 0.00.** 58 M, family history of cluster headache, on a vasodilator. Sudden-onset 10/10 "knife stroke", "violent" pain in the forehead, both eyes and both temples, with tearing. DDXPlus: cluster headache 100%. 11 models rank subarachnoid haemorrhage (I60.9) first; the rest rank cluster headache first with SAH or angle-closure glaucoma in the top 3. Every follow-up asks for an urgent non-contrast CT head or tonometry. GPT-6 Astra: "Sudden 10/10 headache is a red flag despite the intake label." The cluster features are textbook (periorbital, lacrimation, family history, vasodilator trigger), but the form does not say whether this is a first episode, and "sudden" here is the decoder's word for onset code 9 on a 0-10 scale. Faced with "sudden, 10/10, worst pain" on paper and no way to ask, the safe human call is also a CT. Verdict: defensible. The three 7/10 cluster cases (ddxplus_104547, 120434, 31967) are where a human might instead ask one question and seat the patient; verdict debatable.
3. **ddxplus_15697, influenza, escalated 18/18, P(severe) 0.10.** 24 F, immunosuppressed, smoker. Fever, 8/10 pain in the back of the head, neck and forehead, sweats, bed-bound, sore throat, diffuse myalgia, a pink rash over 1 cm on the neck and forehead, coryza, cough. DDXPlus: influenza 14%, URTI 14%, HIV 12%, bronchitis 9%, with Ebola 6% and scombroid 4% as the severe mass. Eight models rank meningitis (G03.9) first, and the follow-ups ask for meningeal signs, a glass test, blood cultures and lumbar puncture. No model mentions Ebola or scombroid, which is what the label's P(severe) is made of. Fever, severe headache, neck pain and a rash in an immunosuppressed patient is the textbook "assess now" combination. Verdict: defensible. The same influenza template in a 58 M who is not immunosuppressed (ddxplus_36629) is debatable: 17 of 18 models still escalate.
4. **ddxplus_101125, HIV seroconversion, escalated 16/18, P(severe) 0.00.** 50 F, injects drugs, multiple partners, an HIV-positive partner. Fever, sweats, lymphadenopathy, 6/10 headache, bed-bound, sore throat, 3-month weight loss, pale ulcers on the gums and labia. DDXPlus: HIV 28%, influenza 24%, Chagas 18%, URTI 18%. Models rank B20 or B23.0 first and ask for a fourth-generation HIV test; Opus 5 and Fable 5 add blood cultures "to assess for sepsis/injection-related endocarditis". Fever in a person who injects drugs is a recognised same-day work-up, but nothing on the form (no rigors, no dyspnea, no chest pain, no confusion) says minutes. Verdict: debatable. The two seroconversion cases without IV drug use (ddxplus_110219, 119501) drop to 10/18 and 8/18 escalations; verdict unnecessary.
5. **ddxplus_115087, otitis media, escalated 5/18, P(severe) 0.00.** 72 M, bilateral 4/10 ear pain and fever. DDXPlus: acute otitis media 100%. Fourteen models say routine care. The five escalations come from Opus 5 ("sepsis screen plus urgent malaria smear, given undifferentiated fever and recent travel"), DeepSeek R1 (malignant otitis externa, mastoiditis), GPT-5.2, Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5. The "recent travel" is the decoding artifact: `E_204_@_V_10` means no travel. Verdict: unnecessary. This is what genuine over-caution looks like on this benchmark, and it is 12% of the events.
6. **ddxplus_1515, GERD, escalated 19/19, 12 CONFIDENT, P(severe) 0.00.** 29 M, obese, drinks heavily, hiatal hernia. Epigastric 5/10 pain radiating to the lower chest, reflux, worse after eating, black stools, and coffee-ground vomiting. DDXPlus: GERD 100%. Every model ranks a peptic ulcer or GI bleed first and asks for vitals and a haemoglobin. This is the clearest label error in the set: DDXPlus generated hematemesis and melena as features of severity-3 GERD, and the scoring label treats the case as a routine reflux visit.

## 5. Separation rules

All rules read only the case's evidence codes, age and (for P and S) the DDXPlus differential. Escalation rate is the mean share of models escalating. Verdicts are the counts from section 3.

Reading the table:

1. **TIER1 is the "a human would do the same" set.** It catches every defensible verdict and no other, and models treat it as urgent 97% of the time. The P(severe) bands do not find it: 12 of the 16 TIER1 cases have P(severe) = 0.00, because the diagnosis a clinician escalates for is outside the DDXPlus universe.
2. **TIER2 is the "reasonable people differ" set.** Eleven of its twelve cases are debatable. The one unnecessary verdict (ddxplus_110219) enters through R6 on a cervical-spine pain location with fever and a rash; a stricter R6 (require `E_91` fever and a head-pain location above the neck) would drop it and ddxplus_31957 too.
3. **What is left is over-caution, and it is small.** 34 cases, 61 events, 12% of over-escalation, an escalation rate of 9.8%. The two debatable cases outside both tiers are the 81-year-old with a new temporal headache and no fever (ddxplus_16646) and the testicular-pain hernia (ddxplus_12868).
4. **Per model, TIER1 accounts for 11-16 of each model's 19-38 over-escalations.** Every model but Gemini 3 Pro (11) and GPT-5 Mini (13) escalates 14-16 of the 16 TIER1 cases. What distinguishes the cautious models (DeepSeek R1, GPT-5.2, Opus 5, Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.6, GPT-6 Astra) is 5-11 escalations outside both tiers, against 0-3 for GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-5 Chat, Kimi K3 and Gemini 3.1 Pro. The per-model split is in `over_escalation_summary.json` -> `per_model_tiers`.

Effect on the triage score if the tiers were counted urgent (`over_escalation_summary.json` -> `relabel_impact`):

| Label | Urgent / non-urgent cases | Mean under-triage | Mean over-triage | Mean triage score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T = 0.15 (current) | 188 / 62 | 9.0% | 43.7% | 30.8 |
| T = 0.15, TIER1 counted urgent | 204 / 46 | 8.7% | 25.7% | 39.2 |
| T = 0.15, TIER1 and TIER2 counted urgent | 216 / 34 | 9.7% | 9.4% | 42.2 |

Under-triage barely moves because models already escalate these cases. Over-triage drops below the O = 35% tolerance once TIER1 is counted urgent, and the ranking changes shape: the confident-style models that escalate every bleed (Opus 5 42 -> 57 -> 81, GPT-6 Astra 37 -> 49 -> 66, GPT-5.2 39 -> 51 -> 67) gain most, and the permissive models that score well now by declining some TIER2 cases (GPT-5 Mini, GPT-OSS 120B, Kimi K3, GPT-5.6 Terra) fall once TIER2 counts as urgent.

**Recommended rule.** Count TIER1 as urgent. It is three lines of evidence codes, it flags nothing a clinician would dispute, and it reclassifies 290 of the 515 over-escalation events (56%) as "a clinician would do the same". Report TIER2 as a labelled band rather than folding it in, because its cases are the ones where clinician review will split. Rerun the board's sensitivity grid with both.

## 6. The reverse check: do models escalate urgent cases for the label's reason?

On the 188 urgent cases the 19 models escalate 3,251 times. In 1,326 of those (41%) the model's top 3 contains the family of the DDXPlus severe driver (MI for "Possible NSTEMI / STEMI", anaphylaxis for anaphylaxis, and so on). In 2,861 (88%) the top 3 contains some time-critical diagnosis. On 95 urgent cases escalated by 10 or more models, fewer than a quarter of the escalators name the driver. Examples (`over_escalation_urgent_reasons.csv`):

1. ddxplus_103014 and 107256, GERD with hematemesis: the label's severe mass is Boerhaave (24% and 18%); 16 escalators on each case name a GI bleed and none names Boerhaave.
2. ddxplus_101756, 104180 and 105781, COPD or TB with significant dyspnea and fever: the label's severe mass is pulmonary oedema or Guillain-Barre at rank 4-6 (6-10%); escalators name pneumonia and COPD exacerbation (severity 3) 17-19 times each.
3. ddxplus_10851, sarcoidosis with dyspnea and a seizure code: the label's driver is NSTEMI at rank 2 (22%); 17 escalators name a seizure disorder, one names PE.
4. ddxplus_1059, 11072 and 112558, viral pharyngitis with fever and hemoptysis: the driver is NSTEMI at rank 2-7; escalators name epiglottitis, pneumonia and hemoptysis.

So the same mechanism runs on both sides of the label. DDXPlus attaches severe co-diagnoses to a symptom set that clinicians and models read for different, often off-list, diagnoses. When the model's reason and the label's reason both say "escalate", the board counts a success; when the model's reason says "escalate" and the label's co-diagnoses happen to be severity 3-5, the board counts an over-escalation. The 14 anemia cases show it cleanly: the same melena-on-NOAC form, escalated 96% and 100% of the time, lands 10 urgent and 4 not.

## 7. Artifacts worth fixing before the next run

1. **"Recent travel to N".** `E_204_@_V_10` is the DDXPlus value for no foreign travel; `inference/symptom_decoder.py` prints its label "N" as a destination. 225 of 250 prompts say "Recent travel to N". 44 of 515 over-escalation follow-ups mention travel, 18 ask the clinic to clarify the destination "N", and three escalators rank malaria. The fix is to drop the line when the value is `V_10`.
2. **"Sudden pain onset".** The decoder prints onset code >= 7 as "sudden" and < 7 as "gradual". Cluster headache attacks are coded 8-9, so the model reads "sudden 9/10 knife-stroke pain": the definition of a thunderclap headache. Printing the number ("onset speed 9/10") would let the model, and a reviewing clinician, see the scale.
3. **Constant fields.** "Symptom Duration: unknown", "Patient-Reported Severity: unknown" and "Noted Red Flags: none" appear in every prompt. The red-flags line is misleading next to a chief-complaint list that contains hematemesis; four follow-ups say so.

## Limitations

1. **The verdicts and the rules were written by the same reader.** I assigned categories and verdicts after reading the dossiers, then wrote rules to match the clinical pattern I saw. The rules are computable from the codes, so their counts are reproducible, but the perfect TIER1-defensible agreement is partly circular. A clinician panel should re-grade the 46 cases blind to the tiers; the 28 TIER1 and TIER2 cases matter most because they carry 88% of the events.
2. **One reader, no examination, no vitals.** The verdicts model a clinician reading the intake form with no ability to ask. A clinician at the desk would ask "first episode?" of every cluster-headache case and "how much blood?" of every hemoptysis case, and the answers would move several defensible verdicts to routine. That is exactly the information the benchmark withholds from the model.
3. **Nineteen of 23 rows.** Opus 4.7, Llama 4 Maverick, o3-pro and Grok 4.20 have no per-case data. GPT-5.4 Mini and GPT-5.6 Luna are read from prediction files rewritten after scoring; Sonnet 4.6 is the N=500 proxy.
4. **Follow-up text is the stated reason, not the decisive one.** The field asks for one question or test, and the mapping from ICD-10 codes to diagnosis families is a prefix table (in the script). Both undercount reasons a model held but did not write.
5. **Small denominators.** Categories hold 1-15 cases and the 62-case non-urgent set is itself a sample. Rates inside a category are descriptive, not estimates.
6. **The time-critical family list is hand-made.** It covers what appeared in these 515 events and the urgent-case reverse check; a code outside it counts as "no time-critical diagnosis named".
