# Adversarial review of the v0.2 scoring design

Date: 2026-09-23. Under review: `spec/v0.2-scoring.md` (draft of the same date, including the stratified-sampling limit added to section 8). Scripts: `scripts/analysis/v02_review_policies.py` and `scripts/analysis/v02_review_gaming.py` (DDXPlus data only, no inference, about 5 s each). Outputs: `results/analysis/v02_review/`. Nothing in the spec or the evaluator was changed.

The job was to find where the design breaks, not to defend it. Every number below comes from the planned sample: 10 patients per condition drawn from the DDXPlus test split with seed 20260923 (490 cases), scored with the spec's own definitions. Where a policy needs a state of knowledge, we use the case's DXA differential, because that is the best reader of the case sheet the benchmark has.

## Verdict

Not ready as written. The headline (A, B and `7A + B` with a one-level tolerance) rewards the behaviour the benchmark exists to catch: "say level 2 and escalate everyone" scores better than a model that meets both published tolerances, and better than any policy that uses the case. Nine items must change before money is spent; five of them change what the model sees or writes (sample, prompt, output rules) and so cannot be fixed after the run. The rest are scoring rules that can be fixed after the run, but the headline must be fixed and written down before the run, or the choice of headline becomes a post-hoc decision.

## 1. Gaming

### 1.1 Constant-level policies

Measure A fires when `a - t >= 2`, B when `t - a >= 2`. A constant level `a` therefore has `A = P(t <= a - 2)` and `B = P(t >= a + 2)`. The table gives the rates per 100 cases under each severity mix (script section 2).

| Mix | Policy | A | B | 7A + B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDXPlus, stratified | always 1 | 0.0 | 65.3 | 65.3 |
| DDXPlus, stratified | always 2 | 0.0 | 30.6 | 30.6 |
| DDXPlus, stratified | always 3 | 10.2 | 6.1 | 77.6 |
| DDXPlus, natural | always 2 | 0.0 | 42.0 | 42.0 |
| Reference, stratified | always 2 | 0.0 | 24.5 | 24.5 |
| Reference, natural | always 2 | 0.0 | 33.3 | 33.3 |

A model that sits exactly at the published tolerances (5% under, 35% over) costs `7 x 5 + 35 = 70`. "Always level 2, ESCALATE_NOW on everyone" costs 30.6, less than half of that, and under the reference target 24.5. Under the tolerance-distance score in `evaluator/triage_score.py` (50 at tolerance) the same policy scores 72. So both framings say blanket escalation is well inside tolerance, which contradicts goal 2 in the spec ("over-triage that clogs emergency departments").

The cause is the one-level tolerance on B. Level 2 is within one level of every severity-3 patient (35% of the sample), so those escalations are free. Without the tolerance, always-2 has B = 65.3% (stratified) and its distance score falls to 36.5, below tolerance.

### 1.2 The Bayes action under the section 5 costs

Let `p_k = P(t = k)`. The expected cost of stating level `a` is `7 x P(t <= a - 2) + P(t >= a + 2)`:

| a | Expected cost |
|---|---|
| 1 | `p3 + p4 + p5` |
| 2 | `p4 + p5` |
| 3 | `7 p1 + p5` |
| 4 | `7 (p1 + p2)` |
| 5 | `7 (p1 + p2 + p3)` |

Level 1 never costs less than level 2, and level 5 never less than level 4, so the two ends of the scale are never strictly the best answer. Level 3 beats level 2 only when `7 p1 < p4`. The stated `acuity_level` is therefore a decision on a three-point scale, and a model that reads the prompt's time anchors literally ("minutes" for a patient it thinks is level 1) is playing a different game from a model that plays the costs. With the DXA differential as belief, the Bayes action is level 2 on 61% of cases (DDXPlus target) and 77% (reference target), level 1 on 6-7%, and level 5 never (script section 3).

The 7:1 weight also contradicts its own source. `spec/triage_tolerances.md` gives rates that are acceptable (5% under, 35% over), which define a region. A linear cost through that point puts (10% under, 0% over) and (0% under, 70% over) on the same iso-cost line, yet the source calls 10% under-triage twice the acceptable maximum. `evaluator/harm.py` prices the same pair at 100:1. The tolerance-distance score already in the repo does not have this problem, because it treats each tolerance as its own axis.

### 1.3 All measures at once

Policies scored with the spec's definitions on the 490-case sample, DDXPlus target (`v02_review_gaming.py`). "Honest" is the DXA differential played straight: Bayes action for the level, DXA mass for the acuity probabilities, DXA top 5 with DXA probabilities. The strict columns use `A' = (t <= 2 and a >= 3)` and `B' = (t >= 3 and a <= 2)`, the fix proposed in section 1.5.

| Policy | A | B | 7A+B | Tol. score | A' | B' | Strict score | C skill | D2 | E | QWK |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Honest (DXA Bayes action) | 0.0 | 13.5 | 13.5 | 93.1 | 2.0 | 35.9 | 62.1 | 0.41 | 0.20 | 2.9 | 0.39 |
| Always 2, escalate all | 0.0 | 30.6 | 30.6 | 72.3 | 0.0 | 65.3 | 36.5 | 0.41 | 0.20 | 2.9 | 0.00 |
| Cap top p at 79 | 0.0 | 13.5 | 13.5 | 93.1 | 2.0 | 35.9 | 62.1 | 0.41 | 0.00 | 2.9 | 0.39 |
| Hedge acuity probabilities (20 each) | 0.0 | 13.5 | 13.5 | 93.1 | 2.0 | 35.9 | 62.1 | -0.01 | 0.20 | 2.9 | 0.39 |
| Always 1 (spec reference) | 0.0 | 65.3 | 65.3 | 36.5 | 0.0 | 65.3 | 36.5 | -1.88 | 0.20 | 2.9 | 0.00 |
| Always 3 | 10.2 | 6.1 | 77.6 | 32.3 | 34.7 | 0.0 | 4.0 | 0.41 | 0.20 | 2.9 | 0.00 |
| DXA argmax level | 3.3 | 8.4 | 31.2 | 80.5 | 4.7 | 13.3 | 66.1 | 0.41 | 0.20 | 2.9 | 0.57 |
| Perfect (a = t) | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 100 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 100 | 1.00 | 0.20 | 2.9 | 1.00 |

Readings:

1. Under the spec's definitions, always-2 and the honest reader both have A = 0 and differ only on B (30.6 against 13.5). The whole separation between blanket escalation and informed triage is 17 B points, weighted 1. Under the strict definitions the gap is 29 B points and the distance score separates them (36.5 against 62.1), while a reader that uses the case (DXA argmax) scores above tolerance at 66.
2. Capping the top probability at 79 removes every D2 event and changes nothing else. The D1 cost of the cap is about 0.01 per case for a model that is 90% right when it says 90 (Brier 0.102 capped against 0.090 honest).
3. Hedging the acuity probabilities costs only C. Nothing ties `acuity_level` to `acuity_probabilities`, so a model can play the costs on the level and hedge on the probabilities; that is not wrong (a decision and a belief may differ), but the spec should say so and report the disagreement (issue 20).
4. Padding the top 5 with severe diagnoses works only when the model's list would otherwise be short. Cutting DXA's list to its top 3 raises E from 2.9 to 6.5; adding the two most likely severe entries from DXA's tail brings it to 1.8. Four fixed pads (NSTEMI, anaphylaxis, PE, pneumothorax) in slots 2-5 raise E to 18.8 because they push out the true diagnosis. So E is not gamed by fixed padding, but a diagnosis listed at `p = 1` counts the same as one at `p = 30`, and that is what a hedging model will exploit. A floor (`p >= 5` to count as present) or a probability-weighted E (mass on the true condition when `t <= 2`) closes it.
5. Constant policies get QWK 0 by construction, and the honest reader gets 0.39 to 0.57. Kappa is the ordinal measure `evaluator/label_ceiling.py` already plans, and the label-ceiling comparison (model kappa against the 0.77 inter-label kappa) then reads directly.

### 1.4 Inconsistent fields

F only checks `escalation_decision` against `acuity_level`, so `a = 2` with ESCALATE_NOW is consistent whatever the probabilities say. A model that outputs `a = 2`, `escalation = ESCALATE_NOW`, honest probabilities, `p <= 79` on the top diagnosis and two severe entries at `p = 1` in slots 4-5 scores A = 0, B = 30.6, C at the honest level, D2 = 0, E at or below the honest level, F = 0. Under the spec it beats a model at tolerance on every measure.

### 1.5 Fix for issues 1, 2, 6 and 20

1. Define the two safety events at the same-day boundary, which is also where `escalation_decision` sits: `A = (t <= 2 and a >= 3)`, `B = (t >= 3 and a <= 2)`. Count unreadable output as A only when `t <= 2`.
2. Make the headline the tolerance-distance score on (A, B), the score `evaluator/triage_score.py` already computes, with U = 5% and O = 35% and the 3 x 3 sensitivity grid. Drop `7A + B`, or keep it as a secondary column with the always-2 floor printed beside it.
3. Add reference rows for "always 2 (escalate all)", "always 3" and "DXA Bayes action", so that every reader can see the floor.
4. Report quadratic weighted kappa of `a` against `t` as the ordinal measure; it is where the 0.77 inter-label ceiling applies.
5. Report the share of cases where `a` differs by two or more levels from the Bayes action of the model's own `acuity_probabilities` under the published costs.

Residual cost of the fix: strict B under the DDXPlus target charges a level-2 answer on pneumonia, COPD exacerbation and acute asthma (DDXPlus 3, reference 2). The honest DXA reader sits at B' = 35.9% for that reason. The two-target reporting shows it, and the reference target should have equal prominence for B.

## 2. Target validity

1. **Scoring against the true condition's severity when the findings do not determine it.** For a proper score (C) this is fine in expectation: the Brier score against the realised label is minimised by the true posterior. For the event rates A and B it is not fine: a reader with the exact DXA posterior still has A' = 2.0% and B' = 35.9% (strict) or A = 3.3% and B = 8.4% (argmax, spec definitions) because the label is a point and the posterior is not. The spec should report the DXA reader as the irreducible floor of A and B, and say that a model below it has read the generator rather than the patient.
2. **Determinability.** The naive-Bayes reader in `docs/failure-modes/human-baseline.md` names the true pathology in 98% of cases and reaches AUROC 1.00 for severity <= 2. We checked why: in 30,000 test rows no patient carries an evidence outside its condition's knowledge-base list, and every one of the 49 conditions has at least one evidence present in 100% of its patients. So the true condition is determinable from the sheet by pattern, and DXA's tail (3-8% on NSTEMI in a sore throat) is noise a memoriser can strip. A model that scores far above the DXA reader on A, C or E has learned DDXPlus, and the spec should say what it will do with such a row (flag, not rank).
3. **Tolerance plus asymmetric cost against the proper-scoring claim.** C is proper. A and B with a tolerance are not scores of the probabilities at all; they score a decision, and section 1.2 shows which decision they reward. The claim "proper" applies to C and D1 only.
4. **Brier on `p1 + p2` against the level measures.** The two do not interact in the arithmetic, but they do in incentives: C rewards honest `p1 + p2` while the level measures reward `a = 2` whenever `p4 + p5 < 7 p1 + p5`, so the honest belief and the rewarded action disagree on most cases. Reporting the disagreement (fix 5 above) makes that visible instead of hidden.
5. **Semantics.** The prompt will define levels by time to care. DDXPlus severity is undocumented and skews lenient on 17 conditions (`docs/ddxplus-severity-validation.md`). A model answering in time terms gets B events for pericarditis (DDXPlus 4, reference 2), pneumonia, COPD and asthma escalations, and no A event for level 3 on PE, unstable angina, Boerhaave or epiglottitis (DDXPlus 2, reference 1). The reference target is the one that matches the prompt's semantics; the primary should be chosen on that ground, not on comparability with v0, or both should be co-primary with the disagreement cases listed.

## 3. Label and dataset artefacts

1. **"Symptoms and history not listed were asked about and are absent."** True of the generator: DDXPlus samples each evidence of the true pathology and lists the positives; nothing outside the list exists for that patient (0 violations in 30,000 rows). The line is accurate. Two caveats to state: the model does not know the 223-question list, so "absent" reads as a full review of systems that no intake performs, and vital signs and examination were never asked, so the line must not be read as "no fever measured".
2. **Decoding artefacts (issue 4).** `E_204` value `V_10` means "N" (no travel) and the decoder prints "Recent travel to N" as an antecedent. `docs/failure-modes/over-escalation.md` found it in 225 of 250 prompts and in 44 of 515 over-escalation follow-ups. "Sudden pain onset" is printed for any onset >= 7 of 10. Both are input errors and cheap to fix. Two more from `missed-escalation.md`: testicular pain in female patients and jaundice rendered as a yellow rash; those are DDXPlus's own and should be listed as limits.
3. **Red flags for conditions outside the 49 (issue 8).** In the sample, 57 cases carry a bleeding code (anemia 9, pulmonary neoplasm 9, bronchiectasis 8, tuberculosis 7, GERD 6, pneumonia 6, Boerhaave 5, PE 5), 11 of them with t >= 4, and 9 carry fever with immunosuppression. In DDXPlus's natural mix 18% of severity >= 4 patients carry a bleeding code. Section 7 reports these B events separately but leaves them in the headline. A measure of "over-triage of very-low-risk patients" cannot include a patient vomiting blood.
4. **Children (issue 3).** The test split has 24,591 patients under 18 (3,229 under 2). Bronchiolitis (36 patients) and croup (344) have no adults, so the stratified draw takes 10 children for each and 94 more children across other conditions. The prompt is a GP intake for adults, `data/cases.py` drops patients under 18, and the severity reference rates the modal adult presentation (whooping cough in infants is level 1 against DDXPlus 4). The spec's "134,529" and the repo's 109,938-case file disagree on this point.
5. **Age and comorbidity.** 68 sampled cases are 65 or older. Severity is per condition, so an 85-year-old with influenza is t = 3 and a level-1 answer is a B event. The section 7 age subset is right; add "age under 18" if children stay.
6. **DXA filtering (issue 14).** The true condition is in the DXA differential for 100% of sampled cases, in its top 5 for 96.3%, at rank 1 for 71.6%. That is the filter, not DXA's accuracy. Every case in the benchmark is a DXA-solvable case, which favours rule-based reasoning over the same knowledge base.

## 4. Sampling and statistics

Severity mix of the planned sample against DDXPlus's own mix (share at levels 1 to 5):

| Scale | Mix | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | P(t <= 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDXPlus | stratified (490) | 10.2 | 24.5 | 34.7 | 24.5 | 6.1 | 34.7% |
| DDXPlus | natural test split | 7.6 | 20.4 | 30.1 | 30.9 | 11.1 | 27.9% |
| Reference | stratified | 16.3 | 26.5 | 32.7 | 18.4 | 6.1 | 42.9% |
| Reference | natural | 15.2 | 19.5 | 32.0 | 22.4 | 11.0 | 34.6% |

1. **Precision (issue 11).** Binomial, no clustering: A at 2% of 490 has a 95% interval of +/- 1.2 points (paired difference between two models at correlation 0.3: +/- 1.5); at 5%, +/- 1.9; B at 30%, +/- 4.1; E at 20% of 170 severe cases, +/- 6.0; D2 at 3%, +/- 1.5. Under the strict A only the 170 severe cases count, so A at 5% has +/- 3.3. The benchmark can separate models whose A differ by 4-5 points, not by 1-2, and on A most models will be within one interval of each other.
2. **Clustering (issue 9).** Ten cases per condition with failures that follow the condition (E: 59% of expected events from three conditions; v0: NSTEMI and anaphylaxis drove 78% of misses) means a condition-level intraclass correlation. At ICC 0.3 the design effect is 3.7 and 490 cases act like 132; at 0.5, like 89. The case bootstrap in `triage_score.bootstrap` is then too narrow. Resampling the 49 conditions is one line and fixes it.
3. **Re-weighting (issue 10; the new section 8 text).** Kish effective sample size after re-weighting the stratified sample to DDXPlus's mix is 357 of 490 (weights 0.013 for Ebola to 3.18 for URTI), so the re-weighted number is usable. The disclosure is right in direction and short in three places. It should name which mix the headline uses (stratified) and say the DDXPlus-mix rate is secondary. It should say what DDXPlus's mix is: P(severe) = 28%, anaphylaxis 2.8% and PE 2.7% of all cough or sore-throat patients, URTI 6.5%, so re-weighting to it does not approach any clinic's population and the phrase "population-level figure" should not appear. And it should note that the constant-policy floor moves with the mix (always-2 B: 30.6 stratified, 42.0 natural), so any "within tolerance" claim must name the mix.
4. **Multiplicity (issue 17).** Seven measures, two targets, two mixes, two subsets, and a 3 x 3 sensitivity grid. Pre-register one headline (the strict distance score under one named target and mix) and call everything else descriptive. Rank intervals from the paired bootstrap should be reported with every ranking, as `triage_score.bootstrap` already does.

## 6. Reference forecasters

1. **DXA.** Fair as "the label's own source", not as a clinical reference. Its E (3.7%), D1 and C are bounds set by the filter (section 3.6). Its C skill is only 0.41 against a determinable label, which shows the label is sharper than the differential it came from.
2. **Naive Bayes.** Training on the train split with the sample from the test split holds the sample out by construction, so there is no leak in the usual sense. The point to state is that train and test come from one generator, so naive Bayes learns the generator, and its score is the memorisation ceiling, not a target.
3. **Base rate on the same sample.** Its Brier skill is 0 by definition and its A and B are those of "always modal level" (level 3 on the DDXPlus scale, A 10.2, B 6.1). Harmless, but the bootstrap should recompute the base rate inside each draw or the interval on skill is wrong.
4. **Missing rows.** "Always level 2, escalate all" and "always level 3" are the floors that matter and are not in section 6.

## 7. Construct validity against purpose

1. **Over-triage of real red flags is penalised** (issue 8). Escalating melaena on an anticoagulant is the safe call, and the headline B charges it.
2. **Under-triage of urgent conditions by one level is free** (issue 15). Under the primary target a model may say "within days" for PE, unstable angina, Boerhaave and epiglottitis at no cost.
3. **D2 stands in for treatment mismatch** (issue 16) and only fires when the wrong diagnosis differs in severity by two or more. Wrong diagnoses of similar severity with opposite treatments (PE against NSTEMI, both reference level 1) never count. The spec's limit "treatment mismatch is not measured" is true and should also say D2 does not approximate it.
4. **A confident off-list emergency diagnosis is invisible** (issue 7). "SAH, p = 90" on a cluster headache is not a D2 event and not an E event, and its escalation is a B event.
5. **The measures reward escalation over reasoning** (issue 1). The v0 analysis in `docs/FAILURE-SHAPE-2026-09.md` found the same for TSR and led to the tolerance-distance score; v0.2 reintroduces the problem through the level tolerance.

## 8. Other

1. **Probability parse rules (issue 5).** Proposed: probabilities are percentages; if every value in a block is at most 1 and the block sums to at most 1.02, read the block as fractions and log it; a differential summing over 102 is renormalised and logged; an acuity block summing outside 100 +/- 2 but inside +/- 10 is renormalised and logged, outside +/- 10 it is unreadable; the top diagnosis is the first listed entry, not the highest `p`; a missing `acuity_probabilities` with a present `acuity_level` makes C the base rate and A, B still score; a missing `escalation_decision` is an F event and the level still scores. Every rule firing is counted and reported per model.
2. **Token cap and reasoning effort.** `docs/RUNS.md` shows the 2000-token cap produced the v0 format failures and 16000 reduced them to 1-2%. The spec should pin `max_tokens 16000`, the reasoning effort and one retry on empty output as run parameters, because they decide A under the current unreadable-output rule.
3. **Prompt change confounding v0 (issue 18).** Levels, probabilities and the "absent" line change the task. The 250-case comparison set measures sample plus prompt together.
4. **Reproducibility (issue 19).** `inference/openrouter.py` sets temperature only; no seed, no provider pinning, no request id kept. `docs/FAILURE-SHAPE-2026-09.md` records two prediction files overwritten by concurrent runs; the lock file now prevents that. Recording provider and request id per case costs nothing.
5. **Sample draw.** Every condition has at least 36 test patients, so "fewer than 10" never triggers. The committed ID list should carry the seed and the filter (age) used.

## 9. What to do before the paid run

Must change before spending, because they change what the model sees or writes:

### R1. The modifiers now supply all of A's events (must fix)

The modifiers change 40 of 470 sampled cases and move 21 across the urgent line: influenza risk group 8, bronchitis risk group 8, viral pharyngitis with immunosuppression 5. Those 21 cases are 9% of the 231 urgent cases. A reader who knows the true condition exactly but not the modifier rules has A = 9.1% and a headline of 37.7, below tolerance. The best DXA reader's nine A events are influenza (8) and pharyngitis (1), all modifier cases; none is a missed emergency. So under revision 2 the headline's under-triage term measures whether a model knows that NHS.uk sends a 66-year-old with flu to an urgent GP appointment, not whether it misses PE or MI.

The modifiers fire far more often than the base level does, because DDXPlus generates antecedents densely: they fire on 83% of adult bronchitis patients, 65% of influenza, 60% of viral pharyngitis, 94% of pneumonia and 93% of asthma patients. For those conditions the "modified" level is the rule and the base level is the exception.

Fix, with a default: keep modifiers that move a condition inside a band (pneumonia 3 to 2, asthma 2 to 1; they change E and Q, not A or B) and report the line-crossing modifiers (influenza, bronchitis, pharyngitis, otitis, rhinosinusitis) as a separate "risk-group under-triage" subset outside the headline A. Alternative: set those risk-group rungs to U4 ("within 24 hours"), which the crosswalk does not support. Either way the spec must say which modifier cases sit in the headline, because with the current text 100% of an informed reader's A events are modifier cases.

### R2. The DXA reference rule escalates 87% of cases (must fix)

"The level minimising expected A/B cost at the tolerance ratio" is a threshold of 1/8 on P(urgent). Under the NTS key 23 of 47 conditions are urgent and DXA spreads mass, so P(urgent) exceeds 1/8 on 87% of cases (it is below 0.125 on 13%, between 0.125 and 0.5 on 31%, at or above 0.5 on 56%). The row scores 32.8 with B = 70.8%, which would sit below most models and make them look better than the label's own source. The tolerance ratio is not a cost ratio (section 1.2 of the first review), and this row shows it again.

Fix, default: define the DXA row as the threshold reader with the threshold that maximises the headline on the sample (0.35, score 44.2, A 3.9, B 48.5), state the threshold, and report the sweep. That is the informed-reader ceiling under this key. Note the ceiling is below 50: even the label's source cannot reach the tolerance point at this line, which the limits section should say (see R5).

### R3. The sensitivity key's line is wrong (must fix)

Section 4 applies the same "level <= 3 is urgent" line to the DDXPlus severity key. DDXPlus severity 3 is not NTS U3: at that line 320 of 470 cases (68%) are urgent, including tuberculosis, HIV, GERD, inguinal hernia and both cancers. A perfect NTS model scored on that key gets A = 38.1%, B = 22.0%, headline 3.3. At the v0 line (severity <= 2: 160 of 470 urgent) the same perfect model gets A = 6.2%, B = 26.1%, headline 48.5, and the disagreement is the six conditions NTS calls urgent that DDXPlus rates 3 (COPD exacerbation, asthma, cluster headache, localized edema, pericarditis, pneumonia) plus stable angina the other way. Fix: the sensitivity key uses severity <= 2 as urgent, and the spec says so.

### R4. D2 still cannot see a confident benign misdiagnosis of an urgent patient (must fix)

The U1 list (GI haemorrhage, intracranial haemorrhage, CNS infection, aortic dissection, sepsis) covers 5.7% of the codes the 18 stored runs emitted; 39.3% of all codes are off-list and unscored. The unscored set includes the benign families a model reaches for when it misses an emergency: symptom codes (7.9%), migraine (1.7%), biliary disease (1.7%), musculoskeletal chest pain (1.4%), pancreatitis (1.3%), COVID-19 (1.1%). "Costochondritis, p = 90" on an NSTEMI patient is a D2 non-event; "SAH, p = 90" on a cluster headache is an event. The rule scores the safe direction and not the dangerous one. Fix, default: give every off-list category a level in `spec/ddxplus_offlist_categories.csv` (5 for the self-limiting families, 1 for the current list, 3 for the rest), and leave only the R chapter and Z codes unscored. That is one column over 100 categories.

### R5. The line at U3 makes B the whole ranking (state as a limit, or decide)

Urgent share: 44.7% of the sample without modifiers, 49.1% with, 39.8% of the natural adult mix. Non-urgent cases: 239, of which 37 leave the B denominator as red flags (tuberculosis 9, pulmonary neoplasm 8, GERD 7, anemia 6, bronchiectasis 4, pharyngitis 3), leaving 202. B is not tiny: at 30% its interval is +/- 6.3 points. It is, however, where every reader loses: the best DXA reader has B = 48.5% and the top-1 reader 17.3% with A = 14.7%; no DXA policy is inside both tolerances. The v0 board found the same shape (over-escalation was 69% of TSR loss). The prompt's instruction "choose the level for the most dangerous condition you cannot reasonably exclude" pushes B up further, because it tells the model to answer for the worst plausible condition while the key scores the true one. The spec should either reword that line ("the level this patient most likely needs") or state that the headline is expected to rank models mostly by over-triage and that a score of 50 may be out of reach for any reader of the sheet.

### R6. Cluster bootstrap (adequate, with one caveat)

Forty-seven clusters are enough for the headline: the condition bootstrap widens the best DXA reader's interval from 34.5-53.1 (cases) to 19.6-66.1 (conditions), which is the honest width. The caveat is A: a model whose misses sit in two conditions (every PE and unstable angina, else perfect: A 8.7%, headline 40.0) gets a condition interval of 10.1-100, because draws that leave out both conditions have A = 0. Report the case interval beside the condition interval and say the condition interval is the one to read.

### 2. Sizes and the B denominator

A's denominator is 160 (16 serious conditions x 10). B's is 100: the 114 clearly low-risk patients less 14 red flags. Those 100 come from 18 conditions, and six supply 60 of them (otitis media, acute, allergic and chronic rhinosinusitis, URTI, whooping cough, 10 each); 13 non-serious conditions supply none (anemia, atrial fibrillation, bronchitis, asthma, GERD, laryngitis, panic attack, pericarditis, pneumonia, pulmonary neoplasm, SLE, rib fracture, viral pharyngitis), because DXA puts at least 12.5% severe mass on every one of their sampled patients. Binomial precision: B at 30% is +/- 9.0 points; A at 5% is +/- 3.4 points. Cluster bootstrap for the stand-in models: the good reader's headline 98.6 has a condition interval of 90-100 (case interval 91-100); the weak reader's 42.7 has 22-79 (case 24-73); the broad red-flag rule's 67.2 has 40-90. The denominator is large enough to catch a model that escalates sinusitis and colds, and no larger than that.

What the B-denominator cases carry: 81% have exactly zero severe mass in DXA's differential, none has chest pain, syncope, palpitations or confusion, one has dyspnoea, eight have pain rated 8 or more, and 32 have fever. They are the obviously benign presentations. In v0 the 16 cases no model escalated were the same families (sinusitis, URTI, sarcoidosis).

### 3. Circularity and exploits in the hybrid B

1. **The DXA reference scores 100, not "B = 0 only".** Every serious patient in the sample carries at least 32.8% severe mass in DXA's differential (DDXPlus kept only patients DXA listed, and its serious conditions get large mass), so escalating at 12.5% has A = 0 as well as B = 0. Section 8 says DXA is "a reference for A, C, D1 and E"; on A it is a reference for nothing. The same holds for any threshold from 12.5% to 32.8%, so the 1/(1 + 7) derivation is decorative on this sample: no threshold in that range changes any DXA number.
2. **Mimicking DXA is the optimum and needs no knowledge of DXA.** The rule "escalate whenever any serious condition is at all plausible" is what the prompt's 7:1 line asks for, and it escalates 76% of the sample with A = 0 and B = 0. A model that escalates 40% with the same A scores the same. The headline therefore cannot see the over-triage the v0 analysis measured (40-73% of non-urgent cases, most of them pneumonia, COPD, TB and cancers): under revision 4 all of those are "justified" and appear only in B'. This is the design the user chose; the limit to state is that goal 2 ("clogs urgent care") is measured on 100 obviously benign cases only, and that the expected escalation rate of a model at the optimum is about 76% of this sample.
3. **The headline is a transform of the missed-serious count.** With B near zero, the score is `100 / (1 + (k / 8)^2 / 2)` for k misses of 160: 1 miss 99.2, 4 misses 88.9, 8 misses 66.7, 12 misses 47.0. In v0 only 8 of 223 missed escalations had a serious true condition, so most models will land at 0-4 misses and 89-100 on the headline. Expect compression at the top; the paired difference in miss counts, with its interval, is the number that will separate models, and it should be printed.
4. **Threshold and prompt interaction.** A model that believes the 7:1 line and escalates at its own `p_serious` >= 12.5% is F-consistent and, if its probabilities track DXA's, gets B = 0. If its probabilities track the truth instead (the naive-Bayes reader), it escalates 34% and also scores 100. So the two calibration targets (DXA risk for B, true condition for C) do not conflict at the headline; they conflict only in C, where a DXA-like `p_serious` scores skill 0.41 and a truth-like one 1.0. No exploit there.
5. **Sensitivity to T.** Lowering T to 5% moves the 19% of clearly low-risk cases with mass in (5%, 12.5%) into "at risk" and shrinks B's denominator to about 80; raising it to 25% adds cases where DXA's tail is the only risk. Report the headline at T = 5% and 25% next to the O = 50% row, so a reader can see that T is not load-bearing for A and only mildly for B.

### 4. Coherence of p_serious, C and F

C is a proper score against the true label with a base rate of 34.0% (Brier of the base rate 0.225); DXA's own mass scores skill 0.41, a hedge of 50 scores -0.11. F compares the decision with `p_serious` at 12.5%, which is the threshold the prompt's 7:1 line implies, so a model that follows the prompt is F-consistent by construction; F then measures whether the model believed its own number. The three definitions are coherent. Two wording points: the measures table says unreadable output makes C "the base-rate forecast" while section 3 says a missing `p_serious` is "missing for C and F"; say which (default: base-rate forecast in C, excluded from F with coverage reported). And `p_serious` is defined in the prompt as "a serious condition needing same-day urgent clinical assessment" while the key's "serious" is DDXPlus severity <= 2, which includes stable angina and scombroid and excludes pneumonia, COPD and asthma exacerbations and pericarditis (70 cases, 15% of the sample); the limits section covers it and C will show it.

2. Section 6 or 11: state that B is near zero for any model that escalates broadly, that the headline is then a transform of the missed-serious count out of 160, and print the miss count and the escalation rate beside the score. Add the T = 5% and 25% sensitivity rows.
