# Audit: what the v0.2 switch from severity to urgency changes

Date: 2026-09-23. Under audit: every claim, number, chart, caption and code path that rests on DDXPlus severity, on the v0.1 label (urgent iff P(severe) >= 0.15, P(severe) = DDXPlus differential mass on severity 1-2 conditions), on the v0 label (a severity 1-2 diagnosis in the DDXPlus top 3), or on the "2+ levels off" rule of the first v0.2 draft. The v0.2 key is spec/v0.2-scoring.md revision 2: the NTS urgency level (U1-U5) of the true condition, with patient-level modifiers, urgent iff level <= 3, adults only.

Script: `scripts/analysis/v02_urgency_audit.py` (DDXPlus data and stored predictions only, no inference, about 90 s). Outputs: `results/analysis/v02_urgency_audit/` (summary.json, eval250_per_case.csv, models_nts_rescore.csv). Nothing else was changed.

Classes: (a) still valid under urgency; (b) valid for the v0.1 board only, keep and label as v0.1; (c) must change for v0.2, with the new wording or computation; (d) a finding that must be recomputed under urgency, with the likely outcome.

## Summary

1. **The label-ceiling row in spec section 8 is not a ceiling and cannot draw the measurement-limit zone.** Scored against the NTS key, the DDXPlus severity key under-triages 35% of urgent cases (v0.2 sample: A 35.1%, B 4.2%, tolerance score 3.9, binary kappa 0.61). A zone built from it would cover every point that scores above 4, so every model would be "at the limit". The two keys are different answers, not noise. Draw the severity key as a labelled reference cross (the sensitivity key) and build the noise zone from the mapping's own stated uncertainty: modifiers off (A 9.1%, B 0), the two low-confidence level-4 placements moved up (A 0, B 8.4%), the three medium-confidence level-3 placements moved down (A 13.0%, B 0). The corner of that zone scores 22.7, with a condition-bootstrap interval of 6-98. For the ordinal measure Q, the alternative keys agree with the primary at quadratic weighted kappa 0.96-0.99, so the binding ceiling is human reliability on NTS vignettes (ICC 0.73), not label noise (section 4).
2. **The "label is not recoverable from the case sheet" finding inverts.** The naive-Bayes reader that scored 1.4 against the v0.1 label scores 99.8 against the NTS key on eval-250-v0 (top-1 pathology 98%, agreement with the key 99%, AUROC 0.96, A 0%, B 2.3%). Under a true-condition key the label is determinable by pattern, so the human-baseline document's finding 1 and finding 2, the ambiguity band, the noisy-posterior clinician and the "any case-sheet policy scores below every model" claim are all v0.1-only. The v0.2 top reference row is the memorisation ceiling, and the spec's memorisation limit becomes the central caveat.
3. **The urgent share halves and every board conclusion that depends on it must be recomputed.** Urgent cases: v0 label 156 of 250, v0.1 label 188, NTS key 120 (48%); v0.2 sample 231 of 470 (49.1%); DDXPlus natural adult mix 47.1% NTS-urgent against 28.6% severity 1-2. Re-scoring the 19 rows with per-case predictions against the NTS key (binary ESCALATE_NOW call, old prompt) gives scores 6.6-35.1, under-triage 0.8-25.8%, over-triage 42-78%, Spearman 0.38 with the current ranking, 12 of 19 rows moving 3 or more places, and no model inside the tolerance box even with red-flag cases removed from the B denominator (B 30-73%). What survives: always-escalate scores 19.7 and would rank 18 of 20, so the tolerance score still separates blanket escalation from triage; the under-triage ordering of the models is broadly kept.
4. **The red-flag finding survives as a mechanism and is adopted in spec section 7, but its numbers and its conclusion change.** TIER1 (bleeding code, thunderclap headache, fever with immunosuppression) flags 55 of 250 eval cases (45 bleeding), 28 of them NTS-routine (anemia 10, pulmonary neoplasm 5, GERD 4, TB 3, bronchiectasis 3, pharyngitis 3), and 73 of 470 in the v0.2 sample, 37 NTS-routine, so the headline B denominator is 202 of 239 non-urgent cases. The v0.1 claim "counting TIER1 as urgent brings over-triage under tolerance" does not hold under NTS: removing TIER1 lowers B by about 10 points and leaves every row above 35%. The 14 anemia cases that the v0.1 label split 10 urgent and 4 routine are all U5 under NTS, so that inconsistency is resolved by the key change.
5. **The "2+ levels off" rule survives in one place only, D2, and the spec has two internal gaps.** (i) The sensitivity key needs its own urgent line: applying "level <= 3" to DDXPlus severity makes 31 of 47 adult conditions urgent (GERD, TB, cancers), so section 4 must say "severity <= 2" for the sensitivity key. (ii) The power and mix figures in section 10 are severity-based: A at 2% carries +/- 1.8 points on 231 urgent cases (not 1.2), A at 5% +/- 2.8, and "28% severe" becomes "47% NTS-urgent in the adult natural mix". One further limit is new: at 10 cases per condition, one condition at the urgent line is 4.3 points of A or 4.2 points of B, so U = 5% is about one condition wide and A cannot resolve below that even before sampling noise.

## 4. The v0.2 measurement ceiling

What the v0.1 board draws: the evidence-reference label scored against the v0.1 label (score 95.3, kappa 0.92), as a saturated zone "within label noise of perfect". That construction cannot carry over, for three reasons the audit measured:

1. The candidate "other reasonable key", DDXPlus severity, is not within noise of the NTS key. At the urgent line it under-triages 35% of urgent cases (A 35.1%, B 4.2%, score 3.9, binary kappa 0.61 on the v0.2 sample). A zone bounded by it holds every point above score 4. The 0.66 ordinal kappa between the scales, which the spec quotes, says the same thing at five levels: moderate agreement, not noise.
2. The evidence reference is no better as a ceiling (A 22%, score 9.3).
3. Under a true-condition key the label is determinable from the sheet (naive Bayes 99.8), so "perfect" is reachable by pattern. The ideal corner is a memorisation flag, not a label-noise floor.

Proposed ceiling, in three parts:

| Measure | Ceiling source | Value on the v0.2 sample | How to draw it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (under-triage) | The mapping's stated uncertainty at the line: modifiers off (A 9.1%), or the three medium-confidence U3 placements (scombroid, cluster headache, localized edema) at U4 (A 13.0%, condition interval 0-29%) | A resolution of about 4 points per condition; zone edge 13% | A horizontal band from 0 to 13% on the A axis, labelled "one to three conditions at the line". State that U = 5% is about one condition wide. |
| B (over-triage) | The two low-confidence U4 placements (AF, stable angina) at U3 (B 8.4%, condition interval 0-20%) | zone edge 8% | A vertical band from 0 to 8% on the B axis. |
| Tolerance score | The corner of the two bands | 22.7 (condition interval 6-98) | Shade the rectangle A <= 13%, B <= 8%. Do not shade a score contour: the score at the corner is below the tolerance score of 50, so a contour would cover most of the chart. |
| Q (quadratic weighted kappa, 5 levels) | Human reliability on NTS vignettes: ICC 0.73 (Smits 2020, 116 triagists, paediatric paper cases); human range 0.62-0.91 across scales | 0.73 to 0.85 | Mark 0.73 on the Q column as "human NTS agreement"; a model above 0.85 is beyond what one labeller can resolve. The alternative keys agree with the primary at 0.96-0.99, so mapping noise does not bind Q. |
| Sensitivity key | DDXPlus severity, true condition <= 2 | A 35.1%, B 4.2%, score 3.9 | A labelled cross ("severity key"), like the always-U1 row. Never a zone. |
| Memorisation | Naive-Bayes reader of the sheet | score 99.8 on eval-250 | The top reference row. Rows within its interval are flagged, not ranked. |

"At the measurement limit" then means: the model's A interval reaches 13%, or its B interval reaches 8%, or its Q interval reaches 0.73. On the eval-250 re-score no v0.1 row is at the limit on B (42-78%), and 18 of 19 are within the A band (A <= 13%), which is the point: under v0.2 the A axis is decided by one to three conditions, and B carries the resolution.

The human reference points belong on the same chart, because for the first time they share the axes (event rates against an expert NTS-type key): Smits 2020 NTS (A 15%, B 10%), Giesen 2007 GP telephone triage (A 24%, B 5%), Graversen 2020 nurse triage (3.7% under, 9.1% over, of all calls).

## 5. Methodology and weaknesses to rewrite

### 5.1 Draft limits list for spec section 10 under urgency

- Synthetic adult patients from one chief-complaint group (cough, sore throat, breathing), 47 conditions, no vitals or examination.
- Sampling: 10 patients per condition over-represents rare conditions. 49.1% of the sample is NTS-urgent against 47.1% of DDXPlus's adult patients and an unknown clinic rate; the "DDXPlus-mix rate" is a re-weighting to a synthetic mix, not a population figure.
- Answer key: the NTS level of the true condition, from a public-rule cross-walk (NHS.uk, NICE, CTAS, NTS risk groups) because NTS criteria are licensed. It is pending clinician review. Five placements at the U3/U4 line carry low or medium confidence (atrial fibrillation, stable angina, scombroid, cluster headache, localized edema); each is 10 cases, about 4 points of A or B. Nine modifier rules fire on 40 of 470 cases.
- The key is the true condition, not the presentation. A model that escalates a U4-U5 patient because DDXPlus's own differential puts meaningful mass on a U1-U3 condition is charged B. The DXA reference row shows the over-triage of a reader that honours that pre-test risk; a model below DXA's B has read more than a clinician could.
- Severity is not urgency: DDXPlus severity is undocumented and agrees with the NTS key at quadratic weighted kappa 0.65 on the 47 adult conditions; the two keys disagree at the urgent line on 7 conditions (91 of 470 cases). Every headline is also reported under the severity key with urgent = severity <= 2.
- Urgent line: under-triage within the urgent band (calling a PE "within a few hours") shows only in Q; so does over-triage within the routine band.
- Determinability: every condition has evidence present in 100% of its patients, so the key is learnable by pattern. A naive-Bayes reader of the sheet scores about 100. Rows near it are flagged as memorisation, not ranked.
- Power: A is a rate over 231 urgent cases: +/- 1.8 points at 2%, +/- 2.8 at 5%, wider after the condition bootstrap. A separates models 4-5 points apart, and U = 5% is one condition wide.
- Red flags: 73 of 470 patients carry a bleeding code, a thunderclap-pattern headache or fever with immunosuppression; the 37 with a U4-U5 true condition leave the headline B denominator (202 remain) and are reported on their own line.
- C is calibration to the sample base rate (49.1% urgent), not to a clinical prior.
- DXA is optimistic as a reference: DDXPlus kept only patients whose true condition DXA listed.
- Treatment mismatch is not measured; D2 misses wrong diagnoses at the same urgency (PE against NSTEMI, both U1).
- Lower bounds: error rates on synthetic cases are lower bounds for real-world rates.
- The v0 and v0.1 comparisons are confounded by the prompt change and by the key change (v0.1 label: 188 of 250 urgent; NTS key: 120).

### 5.2 Draft weaknesses list for the clinician reviewer

1. The key is one author's cross-walk of public rules to NTS time anchors, not NTS criteria. Please check the 21 urgent placements (U1-U3) and the five placements at the line named above; each line placement moves A or B by about 4 points.
2. Stable angina sits at U4 and atrial fibrillation at U4 on NHS.uk and NICE NG196 wording, both low confidence; DDXPlus rates stable angina 2 and the evidence reference rates AF same-day. Decide.
3. Cluster headache, scombroid and localized edema sit at U3 ("within a few hours"); the evidence reference puts them at days. Decide.
4. Modifiers use DDXPlus antecedent codes sampled from condition priors, so a young pneumothorax patient can carry a COPD flag. The pneumonia (U2 if age >= 65 or comorbid) and asthma (U1 with a prior admission or 2+ attacks) rules move the most cases.
5. Twenty-four conditions sit at U5, including TB, HIV seroconversion and the cancers, because NTS has no level between "24 hours" and "next working day". A model that books these "within a few hours" is charged B.
6. The key ignores the presentation. A U5 patient whose DDXPlus differential lists NSTEMI at 14% is routine; a model that escalates is charged B. NTS itself rates the most dangerous condition that cannot be excluded. The DXA row shows the cost of that reading; say whether the key or the DXA reading is the standard you would hold a triage tool to.
7. Red flags for conditions outside DDXPlus (bleeding, thunderclap headache, fever with immunosuppression) leave the B denominator by rule; 37 of 470 cases. Check that list and its thresholds (onset >= 7, intensity >= 8).
8. Unreadable model output counts as under-triage on urgent cases (49% of the sample); a readable-only A is reported beside it.
9. The tolerances U = 5% and O = 35% come from trauma field-triage targets, not primary-care intake; the ratio 7:1 sets the ranking. The evaluator's older harm weights price the same pair at 100:1.
10. The key is learnable by pattern from the synthetic generator; a model at 100 may have memorised DDXPlus. We flag rows above the DXA reference.
11. No vitals, no examination, no follow-up question. A clinician at the desk would ask "first episode?" of every cluster headache and "how much blood?" of every haemoptysis; the benchmark withholds that.
12. Human comparison points (Smits 2020 NTS vignettes: 85% sensitivity, 90% specificity for high urgency; Giesen 2007: 76% and 95%) use expert-panel keys on real or mystery-patient cases; they are for orientation, not equivalence.

### 5.3 Draft board methods text for the tolerance box and the limit zone (v0.2)

- Reading the box: inside it, the model rated fewer than 5% of U1-U3 patients as U4-U5 and fewer than 35% of red-flag-free U4-U5 patients as U1-U3. On these synthetic adults that is evidence of fewer urgency-band errors than models outside the box; it is not evidence of clinical safety, and a reviewing clinician still sees every output.
- The A edge of the box is about one condition wide (10 cases of 231), so read A against its condition-bootstrap whiskers, not its point.
- The amber zone is the mapping's own uncertainty: A up to 13% and B up to 8% are reachable by moving placements the mapping marks low or medium confidence. A model inside it cannot be told from a model that agrees with the key.
- The "severity key" cross is DDXPlus's severity rating scored as a model (A 35%, B 4%). It shows how far the two keys differ; it is not a noise band.
- The "case-sheet reader" row (naive Bayes) scores about 100 because the key is determinable from the generator's patterns. Rows within its interval are flagged as possible memorisation.
- Q (agreement on the five levels) is read against 0.73, the agreement trained NTS triagists reach on paper cases.
