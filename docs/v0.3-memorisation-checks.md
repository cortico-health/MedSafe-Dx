# Memorisation checks for v0.3

Date: 2026-09-25. Scope: the 470-case main sample (`eval-v02-adult`) and the two v0.3 pools (atypical 150, high-risk 100), against the DDXPlus validate and test splits in this checkout (108,372 and 109,938 adults). Code: `scripts/analysis/v03_memorisation.py`; outputs in `results/analysis/v03_memorisation/`. No inference spend. This answers finding 7 of `docs/v0.3-astra-review.md`: audit exact and near duplicates, measure performance without antecedent shortcuts, and prepare meaning-preserving rendering variants.

The question behind all four parts: how well can a reader score by knowing DDXPlus rather than by reading the patient, and how do we tell such a row apart from a clinically strong one? We measure three routes to dataset knowledge. Recalling public rows (part 1). Learning the generator's signature from the symptom line or the history line (part 2). Matching the decoder's surface wording (part 3). Part 4 turns the numbers into a flag rule.

## Summary

1. **15% of the 720 cases have an exact twin in the public rows we hold, and every twin shares the true condition.** Same age band, sex and evidence tokens: 83 of 470 main-sample cases (17.7%), 10 of 150 atypical (6.7%) and 16 of 100 high-risk (16.0%) have at least one twin among the 218,310 public adults in the validate and test splits; 315 twin rows, 315 with the same truth. Near twins (Jaccard >= 0.9) cover 151 cases (21.0%), 1,247 rows, all with the same truth. Twins concentrate on short evidence lists (median 8 tokens against 20) and on conditions with few codes (myasthenia gravis 18 cases, asthma 13, Guillain-Barre 13, laryngospasm 10). The train split (about 839,000 adults, 3.8 times the rows we hold) is not in this checkout; the twin share rose from 10.8% to 15.1% when the searched rows doubled, so the share with a twin somewhere in public DDXPlus is above 15% and a Poisson extrapolation, which under-predicts at the ratio we can check, gives no useful upper bound (section 1).
2. **Row recall does not need exact twins: a nearest-neighbour reader of one public split names the true condition in 97.5% of cases.** The nearest public row by Jaccard (mean 0.77) carries the truth for 702 of 720 cases; the 18 misses are acute against chronic rhinosinusitis and stable against unstable angina, pairs DDXPlus itself barely separates. So a model that recalls public DDXPlus rows approximately sits 2 points under the naive-Bayes ceiling (99.4%) and 28 points above DXA (69.4%) without any medical knowledge (section 1).
3. **The history line alone carries most of the signature: naive Bayes on antecedents only names the truth in 86.0% of main-sample cases and separates tier-1 truths from the rest at AUC 0.994.** Symptoms only: 94.5% and AUC 0.999. All codes: 99.4% and AUC 1.000. DXA: 69.4% and 0.887. DDXPlus samples risk-factor antecedents only for the true condition, so a reader that pattern-matches the history line gains 16.6 points over DXA (interval 6.6 to 27.0) and reaches the serious/benign ceiling. On the headline classification (R10 case against clearly-low-risk case) no reader separates from DXA (naive Bayes 0.932 against 0.890, difference 0.041, interval -0.067 to 0.159), because R10 includes DXA-derived targets the truth does not carry; that AUC cannot serve a memorisation flag (section 2).
4. **Two rendering variants are implemented, tested and ready for a paired run; none has run.** `inference/run_inference.py --rendering shuffled` shuffles the symptom and history order with a fixed per-case seed; `--rendering paraphrased` replaces the 40 most frequent decoder strings (42% of string occurrences; 7.4 replacements per main-sample case) with a fixed phrase table. Tests check that every rendering carries the same findings under prompts v5, v6 and every v7 arm. `docs/v0.3-rendering-variants.md` shows three cases rendered each way and states the reading: a large paired score change across renderings means the row reads the surface, not the findings (section 3).
5. **Proposed flag rule: a row is flagged on a measure when it beats the DXA reader by more than X with the paired interval excluding 0, and sits within Y of the naive-Bayes ceiling.** Top-1: X = 15 points, Y = 5 points. Tier-1-truth AUC of p_serious: X = 0.05, Y = 0.01. X is half the DXA-to-ceiling gap on the sample (30.0 points, 0.113) and equals the history-line shortcut's lift over DXA; Y is the band that only signature readers occupy (naive Bayes 99.4, nearest neighbour 97.5, symptoms-only 94.5; DXA 69.4). Both parts are needed: near the ceiling alone would flag rows on a set where DXA itself reaches the ceiling, and far above DXA alone would flag rows on the atypical pool where DXA scores 0 by construction. The two v6 rows scored so far (58.5% and 47.2% top-1) are 26 and 37 points below the flag band (section 4).

## 1. Duplicate audit

**Method.** For each sample case we take its evidence tokens (a code with its value, for example `E_55_@_V_29`), its sex and its age band (18-39, 40-64, 65+; the bands of `docs/risk-proxy-validation.md`). An exact twin is another public adult row with the same band, sex and token set; a near twin has the same band and sex and Jaccard >= 0.9 on tokens. A case is never its own twin. We search the validate split (108,372 adults) and the test split (109,938 adults, which contains the sample rows). The train split is not in this checkout, so the numbers are what an attacker holding the two smaller public splits could do; section 1.2 says what the train split adds.

**Results, both splits searched.**

| Set | Cases | Cases with an exact twin | Exact twin rows | Twin rows with the same truth | Cases with an exact twin at the same age | Cases with a near twin (J >= 0.9) | Near twin rows | Near rows with the same truth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main sample | 470 | 83 (17.7%) | 246 | 246 (100%) | 5 | 110 (23.4%) | 755 | 755 (100%) |
| Atypical pool | 150 | 10 (6.7%) | 18 | 18 (100%) | 1 | 16 (10.7%) | 137 | 137 (100%) |
| High-risk pool | 100 | 16 (16.0%) | 51 | 51 (100%) | 1 | 25 (25.0%) | 355 | 355 (100%) |
| All | 720 | 109 (15.1%) | 315 | 315 (100%) | 7 | 151 (21.0%) | 1,247 | 1,247 (100%) |

Per split, main sample: 69 cases have an exact twin in the test split (135 rows) and 62 in the validate split (111 rows). Per split, all 720: 90 and 78.

Every twin shares the truth because DDXPlus samples one condition per patient and draws the evidence from that condition's list; two patients with the same list are two draws from the same condition. Twins are therefore not label noise. They are the cases a row-recalling model answers for free. The exact-age count (7 of 720) shows that age is the only field that separates twins, and the prompt prints the exact age, so a model would have to match on the evidence and ignore the age; a near-twin search already does that.

**Who has twins.** Cases with a twin have a median of 8 tokens (without: 20). By condition: myasthenia gravis 18, bronchospasm / acute asthma exacerbation 13, Guillain-Barre 13, laryngospasm 10, whooping cough 9, COPD exacerbation 8, acute dystonic reactions 8, allergic sinusitis 8. These are the conditions whose evidence lists are short, so the generator repeats itself. The atypical pool has fewer twins (6.7%) because its cases are the long, unusual presentations.

**Nearest neighbour, the bound that matters.** A reader that recalls public rows does not need an exact twin. For each case we took the nearest public rows by Jaccard in one split and read off their condition (majority, ties split):

| Set | Mean max Jaccard, validate | 1-NN top-1, validate | 1-NN top-1, test (self excluded) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main sample | 0.767 | 97.4% | 97.2% |
| Atypical pool | 0.699 | 96.0% | 95.3% |
| High-risk pool | 0.778 | 100% | 99.0% |
| All | 0.755 | 97.5% | 97.1% |

The 18 misses in the validate split are 5 rhinosinusitis cases (acute against chronic), 1 laryngitis, and 12 angina cases (stable against unstable, 6 in the main sample and 6 in the atypical pool). Naive Bayes over all codes misses 3 of 470. So the row-recall route reaches 97.5% top-1 from one public split of 108,000 rows, 2 points below the ceiling and 28 above DXA, with no medical knowledge at all. This is the number the flag rule in section 4 has to sit under.

### 1.2 The train split

The share of cases with an exact twin was 10.8% against the validate split alone, 12.5% against the test split alone, and 15.1% against both (218,310 rows). The train split holds about 839,000 adults (1,025,602 rows, the same adult share), 3.8 times the rows we searched. A Poisson reading of the per-case twin counts, which scales each case's count with the rows searched, predicts 60 cases with a test-split twin from the validate counts where 90 are observed, so it under-predicts, because twins cluster on a few short-list cases and the count from one split misses cases whose twins happen to fall in the other. The same reading gives 108 of 720 for the train split, a lower bound. Two statements hold: at least 15% of the cases have an exact twin in public DDXPlus, and the share grows with every split a model has seen. The nearest-neighbour figure does not depend on this: 97.5% from one split can only rise with more rows.

## 2. Shortcut analysis without antecedents

**Method.** The v0.3 naive-Bayes reference (`evaluator/v02_references.py`, `scripts/build_v03_key.py`) uses the presence and absence of each base evidence code, trained on the test split with the 720 sample and pool rows held out (133,809 rows). We retrain it twice with the feature set cut down: symptoms only (the 110 codes with `is_antecedent` false in `release_evidences.json`) and antecedents only (the 113 codes with it true; risk factors, history, travel). Each reader gives a posterior over the 49 conditions; its top-1 is the argmax, its p_serious is the posterior mass on the 21 tier-1 conditions, and it says YES when a tier-1 posterior is >= 10%, as the reference does. The DXA reader is the case's own DDXPlus differential (top-1 by probability; p_serious its tier-1 mass; YES from the reference row, with the red-herring rule). Intervals are the scorer's cluster bootstrap (47 conditions resampled, 2,000 draws, seed 20260923); the AUC per draw weights each case by its condition's multiplicity.

Two serious/benign classifications, both scored by p_serious:

- **Tier-1 truth:** the true condition is tier 1 (`spec/dangerous_if_missed_tiers_v03.csv`; 200 of 470) against the rest. This is the truth-anchored reading, where naive Bayes is a ceiling by construction.
- **Headline classes:** R10 case (234) against clearly-low-risk case (118), the two classes the draft-3 headline scores; intermediate cases are left out. R10 includes DXA-derived targets, which the truth does not carry, so naive Bayes is not a ceiling here.

**Main sample, 470 cases.**

**Pools.** Top-1: all codes 98.7% (atypical) and 100% (high-risk); symptoms only 95.3% and 98.0%; antecedents only 80.7% and 93.0%; DXA 0% (by construction: the pool selects cases whose DXA top diagnosis is tier 3) and 63.0%. Within a pool the serious/benign AUC is undefined (every atypical truth is tier 1, no high-risk truth is), so we pool them: atypical truths against high-risk truths, AUC 1.000 for all codes, 0.998 symptoms only, 0.996 antecedents only, 0.585 for DXA. Every pool case carries an R10 target, so the headline AUC is undefined there.

**Reading.**

- The history line is a shortcut on its own. DDXPlus samples risk-factor antecedents only for the true condition (`docs/dxa-red-herrings.md`, section 1), so "HIV, high cholesterol, smoker, family cardiovascular history" is MI's signature and "active cancer, immobile, recent surgery" is PE's. A reader of the history line alone reaches 86% top-1 and AUC 0.994, 16.6 points and 0.107 AUC above DXA. A model can exploit this line without any symptom reasoning, and the ABSENT_LINE in the prompt ("Symptoms and history not listed were asked about and are absent") makes the absent antecedents informative too.
- The symptom line is a second, independent shortcut: 94.5% and 0.999 on its own. The two lines are redundant for seriousness (each alone reaches AUC >= 0.994), which is why the full reader's AUC is exactly 1.000 with a degenerate interval.
- On the headline classes the ceiling is not a ceiling. R10 against clearly low separates at 0.93 for naive Bayes and 0.89 for DXA, and their difference has an interval that includes 0. The 34 cases whose only targets are DXA-derived are R10 cases that a truth reader answers NO on (spec section 11), so a model can legitimately beat naive Bayes there by also raising DXA's concerns. The flag rule therefore uses the truth-anchored AUC, not the headline one.
- DXA is not a clinical reader, and no reader here is. DXA is the generator's own differential engine; its top-1 is low because it spreads mass across the knowledge base, not because it lacks knowledge. We have no measured ceiling for a clinician reading these sheets. What we can say is which readers sit in which band: dataset-knowledge readers at 94-99 top-1, the generator's differential at 69, and the two model rows scored so far at 47-59.

## 3. Rendering variants

Two meaning-preserving renderings are implemented in `inference/rendering.py` and selected with `run_inference.py --rendering {standard,shuffled,paraphrased}` for prompts v5, v6 and every v7 arm. The rendering is recorded in each prediction and in the run metadata, and a value that differs from the run config flags the row like any other override.

| Rendering | What changes | What stays |
|---|---|---|
| standard | nothing: the v0.2 decoder's strings in DDXPlus's evidence order | |
| shuffled | the order of the symptom strings and of the history strings, each shuffled with `random.Random(f"20260925:{case_id}")`, so a rerun renders a case the same way and cases pair across runs | every string |
| paraphrased | each of the 40 most frequent decoder strings is replaced by a fixed alternative phrase (`PARAPHRASE`); the table is a bijection, so a prompt reads back to its findings | the order, and every string outside the table |

The 40 strings are the most frequent v0.2-decoder strings over the 720 sample and pool cases (`results/analysis/v03_memorisation/frequent_strings.csv`). They are 41.9% of the 13,278 string occurrences; the table replaces 40.2% of the main sample's occurrences (7.4 strings per case, 469 of 470 cases touched), 46.0% of the atypical pool's and 43.1% of the high-risk pool's. `evaluator/tests/test_rendering_variants.py` checks that the multiset of findings is identical under every rendering for every prompt version, that only the two finding lines of the prompt change, that the shuffle is deterministic per case, that the table has 40 entries that each differ from their original and invert cleanly, and that the table's keys are the sample's top 40.

`docs/v0.3-rendering-variants.md` shows three cases rendered each way and states the reading for the paired test: a row whose score changes by more than the flag rule's Y (5 top-1 points, or a headline change with the paired interval excluding 0) between renderings is reading the surface, not the findings. No rendering run has been made; the variants are ready for the prompt-test schedule in spec section 12.

## 4. Memorisation-flag rule

**Proposed rule.** On the main sample, for a scored row r, with the naive-Bayes reference NB and the DXA reader DXA from the same scored board, and paired 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals (conditions resampled, 2,000 draws, seed 20260923):

r is flagged for memorisation on measure m when both hold:

- (A) r_m - DXA_m > X_m, and the lower bound of the interval of (r_m - DXA_m) is above 0;
- (B) NB_m - r_m < Y_m.

| Measure m | X_m | Y_m | Flag band on the 470 (from the reference points) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top-1 true-condition accuracy, strict map, in points | 15 | 5 | above 84.4 with interval, and above 94.4 |
| AUC of p_serious for tier-1 truth against other truth | 0.05 | 0.01 | above 0.937 with interval, and above 0.990 |

A row flagged on either measure is reported with the flag and left out of the ranking, as `docs/v0.2-adversarial-review.md` asked ("flag, not rank"). The flag is defined on the main sample only. On the pools the same measures are reported beside the references without a flag, because DXA's top-1 on the atypical pool is 0 by construction and the within-pool AUC is undefined.

**Why X.** X is half the DXA-to-ceiling gap on the main sample: 30.0 top-1 points (interval 21.5 to 39.4) and 0.113 AUC (0.055 to 0.185). That is the reading the current `memorisation()` in `evaluator/v03_score.py` already uses for top-1 ("more than halfway from DXA to naive Bayes"), fixed here as a number so it is pre-registered rather than recomputed. It is also the lift the history-line shortcut alone gives over DXA (16.6 points, interval 6.6 to 27.0; 0.107 AUC): a row that clears X has gained at least as much over the generator's own differential as a reader that only pattern-matches the antecedents. For AUC, 0.05 sits under the lower interval bound of the gap (0.055), so the ceiling reader itself clears it. The interval condition stops a flag on a difference that the 47-condition sample cannot resolve; the two v6 rows scored so far are 11 and 22 points below DXA, so nothing scored to date is near the band.

**Why Y.** Y marks the band that only readers of the generator's signature occupy. On top-1 they are naive Bayes 99.4 (interval 98.3 to 100), nearest public row 97.5, and symptoms-only naive Bayes 94.5 (4.9 below the ceiling, interval 1.3 to 9.4); the next reader down is antecedents-only at 86.0 and DXA at 69.4. Y = 5 points puts the symptoms-only reader at the edge of the band and everything clinical or partial outside it. On AUC, symptoms-only (0.999) and antecedents-only (0.994) both sit within 0.006 of the ceiling of 1.000 while DXA is 0.113 below, so Y = 0.01 admits every signature reader and nothing else measured.

**Why both.** (B) alone would flag a row on a set where DXA itself reaches the ceiling, where being near the ceiling shows nothing; the high-risk pool (DXA 63, naive Bayes 100) is not such a set, but a future set could be. (A) alone would flag a row on the atypical pool, where DXA scores 0 and any reader clears X. Together they say: the row is far above the generator's differential and at the dataset ceiling.

**What the flag cannot tell, and what the rendering test adds.** The flag does not separate row recall (section 1: 97.5% from one public split) from signature learning (section 2: 99.4% from the code likelihoods); both are dataset knowledge and both belong under the flag. The rendering test separates surface reading from semantic reading: a flagged row whose top-1 falls by more than Y (5 points) under shuffling or paraphrase, with the paired interval excluding 0, reads the decoder's surface; a flagged row that holds has learned the signature in meaning, which is still dataset knowledge and still flagged.

**Changes this asks of the scorer** (not made here): report the tier-1-truth AUC of p_serious as a descriptive measure with its interval; replace the OR in `memorisation()` by the two-part rule above with X and Y as constants; print the flag band beside the references.

## 5. Limits

1. The train split is absent, so the twin counts are lower bounds on public DDXPlus, and the nearest-neighbour figure comes from one split.
2. Age bands, not exact age, define a twin; the prompt prints the exact age. The exact-age twin count (7 of 720) shows that a memoriser must match on evidence and ignore age, which a nearest-neighbour search does.
3. The naive-Bayes variants keep the reference's conditional-independence model; a stronger learner would only raise the antecedents-only figure.
4. The serious/benign AUC scores p_serious, which the v7 arms do not all ask for; where a row has no p_serious the AUC part of the flag is not evaluable and the top-1 part stands alone.
5. The paraphrase table covers 40 strings; the other 423 keep the decoder's wording, so a paraphrase-insensitive row may still read those. The shuffle covers every string.
6. Nothing here measures a clinical reader. The band between DXA (69) and the flag band (84) is where a strong reader that is not memorising could sit; the rule leaves it unflagged by design.
