# Cost of escalating a serious patient for the wrong serious condition

Date: 2026-09-26. Scope: the third outcome the v0.3 headline does not yet price - a SERIOUS case the model escalates, naming a different serious condition than the truth (oesophageal rupture flagged as MI, Guillain-Barre flagged as stroke, PE flagged as MI). Current rule (`evaluator/v03b_score.py`): a SERIOUS case not escalated costs 7; a BENIGN case escalated costs 1. The proposal on the table: the wrong-serious outcome costs 1; earlier alternatives were 2 and 3.5. No scoring code, spec or existing file changed.

Method: the session's web-search budget was spent, so we reached every source by direct URL - PubMed E-utilities for abstracts, Europe PMC for the Hautz 2019 full text. Every figure below comes from a fetched abstract or full text, except the rows marked "via docs/dangerous-if-missed-labels.md", which that document verified on 2026-09-24 and we reuse. We could not retrieve two candidate sources and say so in section 7.

## Summary

1. **Four cohorts of patients admitted from the ED under a diagnosis that later changed put the mortality of "escalated under the wrong diagnosis" at 2.3 to 3.6 times that of a concordant diagnosis, an absolute excess of 3.6 to 5 percentage points, with 1 to 3.4 days longer stay.** Hautz 2019 (755 patients): 8.6% vs 3.8% died, adjusted OR 2.49 (95% CI 1.06-5.86). Eames 2016 (6,281): 5.1% vs 1.5%, RR 3.35. Bastakoti 2021 (418): OR 3.64 (1.03-12.9). Ovesen 2022 (dyspnoea admissions): 30-day 10% vs 5%, adjusted OR 2.32 (1.08-4.96). These cohorts pool every kind of change, including a benign label that turned serious, so they bound our outcome from above (section 2A).
2. **Against the miss anchor, that harm is about a tenth.** Newman-Toker 2023 puts serious harm (death or permanent disability) per diagnostic error in dangerous disease at 0.40-0.63 (via docs/dangerous-if-missed-labels.md section 7); the discordance cohorts put excess death per wrong admission diagnosis at 0.04-0.05. On a scale where the miss costs 7, a tenth is 0.7, which rounds to 1. Hautz's adjusted interval (OR 1.06-5.86 on a 3.8% base) spans 0.2 to 15 percentage points of excess death, so the same arithmetic spans 0.03 to about 2 (section 4).
3. **The delay to the correct diagnosis after arrival is hours to a few days, not the weeks a discharged miss can run.** Aortic dissection first labelled ACS: 51 vs 15 hours to diagnosis (Hansen 2007). IRAD: median 4.3 hours from ED arrival to diagnosis, upper quartile 24 hours (Harris 2011). Admitted ED patients with a process error: correct diagnosis 1 to 16 days after admission (Chellis 2001). Asymmetric Guillain-Barre first taken for stroke: median diagnostic delay 3 vs 2 days (Zhang 2026). Compare PE misdiagnosed anywhere: median delay 6 days, misdiagnosis in 50% (Alonso-Martinez 2010) (section 3).
4. **Active harm from the wrong label is pair-specific, and the pairs that matter run through either an antithrombotic given for a bleeding-prone truth or a surgical truth parked on a medical pathway.** Dissection labelled ACS: aspirin in 100%, heparin in 85%, lytics in 12% of misdiagnosed patients; major bleeding 38% vs 13%, in-hospital death 27% vs 13% (Hansen 2007, n=66); two larger series found no in-hospital mortality difference (Pourafkari 2017: 48.9% vs 43.7%; Kurabayashi 2011: 18% vs 15%). Oesophageal perforation treated after 24 hours: death 20.3% vs 7.4%, RR 2.28 (Biancari 2013, 75 studies). PE labelled ACS is a low-harm pair because heparin treats both: ED heparin 1.4% vs 6.7% in-hospital death (Smith 2010). Thrombolysing a stroke mimic: symptomatic haemorrhage 0.5% (Tsivgoulis 2015) (section 5).
5. **Over-triage in the largest cohort costs minutes, not lives: over-triaged low-acuity adults waited 3 minutes longer for orders and spent 42 minutes longer in the ED (Sax 2026, 5.3 million encounters), and over-triage is 9 times as common as under-triage (28.9% vs 3.3%, Sax 2023).** So a flat cost of 1 equates a small mortality excess (wrong-serious) with a resource cost (over-triage). The evidence supports the equation in magnitude - both sit about an order of magnitude below the miss - but not in kind (section 4).

**Recommendation: cost 1 as the primary, with sensitivity rows at 2 and 3.5 and one pair-specific row (Boerhaave escalated under a non-surgical label at 3.5).** Evidence strength: moderate for the order of magnitude, weak for the exact number; no study measures our construct directly (section 6).

## 1. What the benchmark measures, and what the literature measures

The model acts as a clinician's assistant. Its output for a SERIOUS case falls into three bins:

| Bin | What the patient gets | Current cost | Proposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not escalated, or escalated with no serious reason | No urgent workup; the miss runs until the patient returns or deteriorates | 7 | 7 |
| Escalated, wrong serious condition named | Urgent workup aimed at the wrong target; the true condition is found when that workup fails or the patient worsens | 0 (counts as escalated) | 1 (alternatives 2, 3.5) |
| Escalated, right condition named | Urgent workup aimed at the right target | 0 | 0 |

The literature has no study of exactly this construct. The closest constructs, and the direction each one's bias runs:

1. **ED-to-inpatient diagnostic discordance cohorts** (section 2A). Every patient was admitted, so every discordant case was "escalated under a label that later changed". Two biases push these figures above our outcome: the pool includes benign or symptom labels that turned serious (Hautz's example is "acute on chronic obstipation" that became acute pancreatitis), which is closer to a miss with admission than to a wrong serious label; and the wrong label was the treating team's, not an assistant's suggestion that a clinician screens. One bias pushes them below: they count in-hospital or 30-day death only (Pourafkari 2017 found the excess in long-term, not in-hospital, mortality).
2. **Condition pairs** (section 2B). Series of one truth condition first labelled as another. These isolate the mechanism of harm (wrong drug, wrong pathway, delay) but are small, single-centre, and in the thrombolysis case drawn from published case reports.
3. **Harm after escalation in general** (section 2C). Inpatient and ICU diagnostic-error studies show that the acute-care setting catches many, not all, wrong labels.
4. **Anchors** (section 2D). Newman-Toker 2023 and Pope 2000 for the miss; Sax 2023 and 2026 and DiLena 2026 for over-triage.

## 3. Question 1: what happens under a wrong initial serious diagnosis

**Outcomes.** All four discordance cohorts with a mortality outcome agree in direction and size: in-hospital or 30-day death 2.3 to 3.6 times the concordant rate, absolute excess 3.6 (Eames), 4.8 (Hautz) and 5 (Ovesen) percentage points; ICU admission or up-triage 2.6 to 5.5 times; stay 1 to 3.4 days longer. Hautz's adjustment for age, sex and Charlson index moved the OR from 2.40 to 2.49, and the discrepant patients were not sicker on triage category, Charlson or medication count, so the comorbidity explanation does not hold in the one prospective cohort that tested it. The residual explanation is the one Hautz found: the presentation was atypical for the label assigned (OR 3.04), which is also what makes these cases hard, so part of the excess belongs to the case, not the label.

**Delay.** Once the patient is in acute care, the correct diagnosis follows in hours to days: dissection 51 hours under a wrong label vs 15 hours (Hansen); IRAD median 4.3 hours, upper quartile 24 hours (Harris); process-error cases 1 to 16 days after admission (Chellis); asymmetric Guillain-Barre 3 vs 2 days (Zhang). The pre-hospital and discharge-home delays are longer: PE median 6 days from symptom onset (Alonso-Martinez), primary-care PE delay over 7 days in 26% (Hendriksen 2017, BMJ Open 7:e012789, PMID 28279993), Boerhaave median 4 days including referral (Teh) and 16 days in a referral-biased series (Harikrishnan).

**Errors are caught, not always.** Auerbach's 23% error rate among inpatients who died or went to ICU, with 6.6% of deaths attributed in part to error, shows that being in hospital does not end the risk; Raffel's 5.6% among 7-day readmissions and Gunderson's 0.7% harmful errors per admission show the base rate is low across all admissions. The acute-care setting catches most wrong labels because the workup for the wrong serious condition (ECG, troponin, CT, bloods) usually exposes the true one; Kurabayashi's misdiagnosed dissections had fewer imaging studies (0.82 vs 1.53), which is the mechanism by which a wrong label delays.

## 4. Question 2: closer to over-triage, under-triage, or condition-dependent

| Outcome | Harm measure | Magnitude | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missed serious condition (cost 7) | Serious harm per diagnostic error in dangerous disease | 0.40-0.63 | Newman-Toker 2023 |
| Escalated under a wrong diagnosis (unpriced) | Excess in-hospital or 30-day death per discordant admission | 0.036-0.05 (Hautz adjusted CI: 0.002-0.15) | Hautz, Eames, Ovesen |
| Over-triage of a low-acuity patient (cost 1) | Delay to orders; ED stay | 3 minutes; 42 minutes | Sax 2026 |

The two figures in the first two rows are different constructs (any serious harm across all settings vs in-hospital death among admitted patients), as docs/dangerous-if-missed-labels.md section 7 already noted, and the second is an upper bound for our outcome for the reasons in section 1. Read together they put the wrong-serious harm at about a tenth of the miss. On the 7-point scale that is 0.7; the Hautz interval spans 0.03 to about 2. The answer to question 2 is therefore: **in magnitude, closer to over-triage than to under-triage; in kind, a small mortality cost rather than a resource cost; and condition-dependent at the pair level**, where two mechanisms move it toward the miss:

1. **The wrong label triggers a treatment that harms the true condition.** Antithrombotics for a dissection: bleeding 38% vs 13%, death 27% vs 13% in Hansen; lysis with 64% mortality in the published cases von Kodolitsch reviewed. The two larger series (Pourafkari, Kurabayashi) show no in-hospital mortality difference, so the effect is real for bleeding and uncertain for death.
2. **The wrong label parks a surgical condition on a medical pathway.** Oesophageal perforation treated after 24 hours: death 20.3% vs 7.4% (Biancari), 19% vs 0% (Vallbohmer). Here the wrong label costs the one thing that halves mortality, and the delay it buys (days, per Teh) is long enough to matter.

Two mechanisms move it toward zero:

3. **The wrong label triggers the right treatment.** PE labelled ACS receives heparin, the PE treatment; Smith shows ED heparin cuts in-hospital death from 6.7% to 1.4%. Guillain-Barre or myasthenia labelled stroke reaches a neurology bed with monitoring; the cost of the label is a day of delay (Zhang) and a 0.5% sICH risk if thrombolysed (Tsivgoulis).
4. **The workup for the wrong label reveals the truth.** A dissection label orders CT angiography of the chest, which shows a PE, a pneumothorax or mediastinal air.

## 5. High-risk pairs

Aortic dissection and stroke are not DDXPlus truth conditions, so the dissection-labelled-ACS direction, the best-documented harmful pair, cannot occur as a truth case in this benchmark. It can occur only in reverse (MI, PE or Boerhaave labelled dissection), where the label withholds antiplatelets pending imaging and orders the CT that finds the truth. The pairs that can occur, by tier-1 truth condition:

| Truth (DDXPlus, tier 1) | Wrong serious label the model may name | What the label triggers | Harm mechanism | Evidence | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boerhaave | MI or ACS, unstable angina | Aspirin, heparin, cardiology pathway; no surgical referral | Antithrombotics into a mediastinal leak; surgery delayed past 24 h | Biancari RR 2.28; Vallbohmer 19% vs 0%; Khoury: 2 pancreatitis and 1 oesophagitis among 35 thrombolysed non-MIs, no major haemorrhage | Toward the miss |
| Boerhaave | Pneumonia, PE, pancreatitis, pneumothorax | Antibiotics, anticoagulation or a chest drain; no surgical referral | Delay past 24 h; anticoagulation of a leak | Biancari; Harikrishnan (11 of 16 first managed as non-oesophageal) | Toward the miss |
| Pulmonary embolism | MI or ACS | Aspirin, heparin, troponin, angiography | Heparin treats the PE; angiography is a detour | Smith: ED heparin 1.4% vs 6.7% death | Toward zero |
| Pulmonary embolism | Pneumonia, sepsis | Antibiotics; no anticoagulation | Anticoagulation delayed until the admission workup (21.5% of PEs in Torres-Macho) | Smith (aPTT after 24 h: 30-day death 14.8% vs 5.6%); Torres-Macho | Middle |
| Guillain-Barre, myasthenia gravis | Stroke | CT, possible thrombolysis, neurology bed | One day of delay; sICH 0.5% if lysed; respiratory monitoring usually present on a stroke unit | Zhang; Tsivgoulis | Toward zero |
| MI, unstable angina | PE, aortic dissection | CT angiography before antiplatelets or catheterisation | Reperfusion delayed by the scan; heparin given anyway under a PE label | No direct source found; IRAD and Harris describe the reverse delay only | Small, unverified |
| Anaphylaxis, epiglottitis, laryngospasm | Asthma, PSVT, croup, each other | Bronchodilators or adenosine instead of adrenaline or airway control | Wrong first drug in an airway emergency | No source found in this session | Unknown; flag for a later check |

Two pairs merit special handling if the design ever prices pairs: **Boerhaave escalated under any non-surgical label** (10 of 470 adult cases can produce it; the delay evidence is a meta-analysis) and, if aortic dissection ever enters the truth set, **dissection escalated as ACS**. The airway group has no evidence either way in this session.

## 6. Recommendation

| # | Row | Cost for "escalated, wrong serious condition" | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Primary | 1, flat | About a tenth of the miss on the discordance cohorts (0.7 rounded), and the cohorts overstate our construct (section 1). Simplest rule; matches the user's proposal |
| 2 | Sensitivity | 2, flat | Upper end of Hautz's adjusted interval; treats the assistant's wrong label as no better than the treating team's |
| 3 | Sensitivity | 3.5, flat | Half a miss. Not supported by the cohorts; included because it was a live alternative and shows how much the ranking depends on the choice |
| 4 | Sensitivity | 1 flat, except Boerhaave escalated under a non-surgical label at 3.5 | The one pair the benchmark can produce where the wrong label removes the treatment that halves mortality (Biancari RR 2.28). Moves at most 10 cases |

The evidence supports 1 as a defensible primary. It does not support 3.5 as a primary: that would say a wrong serious label carries half the harm of a miss, and every cohort that measured it puts the figure nearer a tenth, before correcting for the benign-to-serious cases the cohorts include. It does not support 0 either: the cohort excess survives adjustment, and Auerbach shows in-hospital error still kills.

Report the four rows beside the headline, as `docs/tier-upgrade-sources.md` does for the tier rule. Label the cost a convention bounded by Hautz and Newman-Toker, not an evidence-based weight; the 7:1 ratio it sits on is itself a convention.

Strength of evidence: **moderate for the order of magnitude** (four cohorts in three countries agree on 2-3.6x mortality, and the direction of their bias is known), **weak for the exact number** (no study isolates serious-to-different-serious discordance; the pair series are small), and **strong for the qualitative claim that harm is pair-specific** (antithrombotic and surgical-delay mechanisms are each backed by a meta-analysis or a consistent series).

## 7. Limits

1. No study measures our construct. The discordance cohorts pool benign-to-serious, serious-to-benign and serious-to-serious changes; only Hautz's Table 1 separates "hierarchically different" (25) from "diagnostically different" (68), and neither is our category. A study that isolated admissions under a wrong serious label would replace section 4's arithmetic.
2. Confounding by case difficulty. Hautz adjusted for age, sex and Charlson only; the atypical-presentation predictor (OR 3.04) means harder cases both get wrong labels and do worse. Eames, Bastakoti and Ovesen are coded-diagnosis or single-centre retrospective cohorts.
3. The cohort label is the treating team's; the benchmark's is an assistant's suggestion a clinician screens. This makes the cohorts an upper bound, by an amount no study measures.
4. Full text was read for Hautz 2019 only; every other figure is from the abstract. Ackfeld 2026 (the largest recent prospective cohort) reports no mortality figure in its abstract, and MDPI returned HTTP 403 for the full text. McGillicuddy 2006 (Guillain-Barre in the ED, Ann Emerg Med 47:390-3, PMID 16546626) carries no abstract in PubMed, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar, so we did not use it. Kamp 1994 (Am Heart J 128:1234-7, PMID 7985606) was not retrieved. Torres-Macho's full text may report mortality by group, which would give the PE-specific admitted-under-a-wrong-label figure.
5. The dissection thrombolysis figure (64% of 34 cases) comes from published case reports and overstates the risk. The two larger series found no in-hospital mortality difference; the bleeding difference in Hansen is the robust finding.
6. Web search was unavailable, so the search ran on PubMed only. Case series on the airway pairs (anaphylaxis or epiglottitis under a wrong label) may exist and were not found.
7. Aortic dissection and stroke, the two best-documented harmful wrong-label targets, are not truth conditions in DDXPlus, so the benchmark cannot show the harm the pair literature describes; the pair table in section 5 is the part of that literature that transfers.
