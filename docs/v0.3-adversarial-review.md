# Adversarial review of the v0.3 scoring design

Date: 2026-09-24. Under review: `spec/v0.3-scoring.md` draft 1 (commit 07f98b2). Scripts: `scripts/analysis/v03_review_*.py` (DDXPlus data, the committed red-herring cells and the v0.1 outputs; no inference, about a minute each). Outputs: `results/analysis/v03_review/`. Nothing in the spec, the evaluator or any existing file was changed.

The job was to find where the design breaks, not to defend it. Every number comes from the spec's own definitions, rebuilt from committed files (`v03_review_common.py`), on the 470-case sample. Where a policy needs model behaviour we use the 19 v0.1 rows on the 250-case v0 set, with ESCALATE_NOW standing in for `serious_concern` = YES and the top-5 differential standing in for `flags`. The v0.1 prompt asked a different question, so those rows show mechanics, not a forecast.

## Verdict

Not ready as written. Two things break the headline before any model is run: "always YES" scores a perfect H with the same coverage as the informed DXA reference, and nothing in the ranking stops it; and the spec's own target counts (227, 247, 120) come from a narrower key than the spec describes, from a per-case file that carries no red-herring labels in the 5-10% band. Both are cheap to fix. Three more items change what the run measures (related-code matching, the invisible "YES with benign flags", the 1% floor at 5-10%) and must be pre-registered before money is spent, because they cannot be fixed by rescoring without a change to what counts as a flag.

## 1. Gaming

`v03_review_gaming.py`, 470 sample, spec key under the text's reading (21 tier-1 conditions, all filtered). Fixed lists use the map's canonical codes, so lenient and strict matching agree. `gaming470.csv` has every column.

| Policy | H | 95% CI | H truth-only | COV | OC | MT truth | MT DXA | YES rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Always YES, no flags | 0.0 | 0-0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| Always YES, fixed top-5 by R5 frequency | 0.0 | 0-0 | 0.0 | 65.5 | 100 | 40.0 | 20.2 | 100 |
| Always YES, fixed top-5 tuned (greedy cover) | 0.0 | 0-0 | 0.0 | 72.1 | 100 | 35.0 | 14.3 | 100 |
| Always NO | 100 | - | 100 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 100 | 100 | 0 |
| YES if chest pain or dyspnoea; fixed top-5 | 27.4 | 16-40 | 27.5 | 65.5 | 25.4 | 40.0 | 20.2 | 53 |
| YES if chest pain, dyspnoea, cough or wheeze | 18.8 | 10-30 | 20.0 | 65.5 | 57.6 | 40.0 | 20.2 | 70 |
| YES if age >= 40 | 35.9 | 29-44 | 37.0 | 65.5 | 58.5 | 40.0 | 20.2 | 62 |
| YES if age >= 65 | 80.3 | 76-85 | 80.0 | 65.5 | 22.9 | 40.0 | 20.2 | 20 |
| YES unless age < 40 and no chest pain or dyspnoea | 10.3 | 6-15 | 10.5 | 65.5 | 72.0 | 40.0 | 20.2 | 83 |
| DXA reader, spec section 8 (10%) | 19.2 | 11-28 | 22.5 | 66.8 | 0.0 | 23.0 | 0.0 | 40 |
| DXA reader at 5% | 0.4 | 0-1 | 0.5 | 98.5 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 57 |
| Perfect (YES iff R10; flags = R5) | 0.0 | 0-0 | 0.0 | 100 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 50 |

Readings:

1. **H alone cannot tell blanket concern from safety.** Always YES has H 0, and a fixed list of five codes gives it the DXA reader's coverage. The only column that separates them is OC (100 against 0), and OC is descriptive. On the v0.1 stand-ins (section 4) the YES rate is already 74-88% under a prompt that asked for same-day escalation; the v0.3 prompt asks for anything "potentially serious", so the YES rate will rise and H will sit near 0 for most rows. The board then ranks on noise.
2. **Coverage is gameable up to the cap.** A fixed list of 5 reaches COV 65.5 (72.1 tuned) because five conditions supply 191 of the 414 R5 target slots: PSVT 46, asthma 40, pulmonary neoplasm 39, MI 35, stable angina 31 (`v03_review_extra.py`, section a). Beyond 5 the cap stops the list; the greedy curve is 18, 34, 46, 72 for k = 1, 2, 3, 5. MT separates (fixed list 40 and 20 against the reader's 23 and 0), but MT is descriptive too.
3. **Symptom and age rules trade H for OC along a line the headline ignores.** Chest pain or dyspnoea: H 27, OC 25. Any chest or respiratory symptom: H 19, OC 58. "YES unless young and no chest symptom": H 10, OC 72. Age alone is useless (DDXPlus has no age gradient, `docs/risk-proxy-validation.md`).
4. **Is descriptive OC enough? No.** The minimal fix that keeps "heavy penalty only for the wrong call": pre-register an OC tolerance (35% provisional, the v0.2 O; 50% as a sensitivity), print it beside H, and mark rows above it as "blanket concern, not ranked". The penalty stays on H; the tolerance only says which rows are eligible for a rank. A two-axis plot (H against OC, with the reference rows and the tolerance line) makes the trade visible without a combined score. Under that rule the chest-pain-or-dyspnoea policy is still ranked (OC 25) with H 27, which is the honest outcome for a rule that ignores the case.

## 2. Target validity

`v03_review_key.py`; per-target file `key470_targets.csv`.

### 2.1 The spec's counts and the committed file

Our M1' rebuild from `cells.csv` and `hallmarks.csv` agrees with `sample470_cases.csv` on all 422 non-truth pairs at >= 10% (labels, hallmark counts, cell sizes and rates). It disagrees on 834 of 1,069 pairs at 5-10%, because the committed file labels nothing in that band: the detector was run at X = 10 and `red_herring` is False for every 5-10% pair.

| Key reading | R10 cases | R5 cases | Clearly low-risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| DXA-derived targets from the 17 severity <= 2 conditions, M1' at 5-10% recomputed | 227 | 247 | 120 |
| The same from `sample470_cases.csv` as committed (no labels at 5-10%) | 227 | 339 | - |
| Spec text: all 21 tier-1 conditions, filtered | 234 | 269 | 118 |
| All 21, the 4 upgraded ones unfiltered | 263 | 317 | 93 |

The spec's 227 and 247 are the first row. The text describes the third. The difference is 44 cases whose R10 set changes, mostly pneumonia at >= 10% in tuberculosis, URTI, bronchiectasis, Ebola and COPD patients (32 of the 44 added targets), and pneumonia as a DXA-derived target is filtered to nothing by M1' (its 5-10% hallmark-1 cell is 559 of 559 true, so it survives only where the truth is pneumonia). Whichever reading is chosen, the per-case target list has to be built at X = 5, committed and hash-pinned, or the key is not pre-registered.

### 2.2 The 5-10% band

DXA-derived pairs at 5-10% on the 470: 1,050; M1' removes 920, none are undetermined, 130 are kept in 102 cases (R5 adds 35 cases that have no R10 target). The kept pairs have 1 hallmark in 48 cases, 2 in 70, 3-4 in 12. Their reference-class truth rate has median 5.3% and lower quartile 1.4%; 53 of 130 are under 2%, 63 under 5%. The tier-1 conditions' 5-10% hallmark-1 cells sit at 1.2-1.7% for GBS, pulmonary neoplasm, pancreatic neoplasm, PSVT and stable angina, and at 0.0-0.7% for Ebola, asthma, laryngospasm, pneumothorax, Boerhaave, epiglottitis, scombroid, myocarditis and MI. The 1% floor is what keeps the first group. The spec's justification quotes a 4.2% median for 5-10% concerns and 2% published rule-out thresholds; a 2% floor in that band is the consistent choice and keeps 77 targets in 67 cases. The detector was designed at >= 10%; its doc reports that at X = 5 it labels 148 true pairs as red herrings against 57 at X = 10, so both errors grow in this band.

### 2.3 What survives, and whether a clinician would agree

Kept DXA-derived R10 targets (71 at 10-20%, 13 above) by count: PSVT 19, MI 13, pulmonary neoplasm 9, unstable angina 8, stable angina 8, myocarditis 6, GBS 5, pneumothorax 5. Only 2 have no hallmark; 97 of the 214 kept R5 targets rest on a single hallmark token. The pairs a clinician would question:

| Truth | Target | Hallmarks | Cases | Cell truth rate | Why it survives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anaphylaxis | PSVT | 0 | 1 | 9.2% | DDXPlus produces PSVT under this presentation; no PSVT symptom is present |
| Anaphylaxis | Pancreatic neoplasm | 1 | 1 | 8.1% | one pain-location token |
| Scombroid | PSVT | 1 | 5 | 11.4% | palpitations |
| Myocarditis | Stable angina | 1 | 3 | 1.3% | exertional symptoms; the 1% floor |
| Pericarditis | Spontaneous pneumothorax | 1 | 2 | 2.1% | one token |
| Acute laryngitis | Epiglottitis | 1 | 2 | 2.7% | hoarse voice |
| Spontaneous rib fracture | Boerhaave | 1 | 2 | 4.9% | vomiting |
| Tuberculosis | Pulmonary neoplasm | 1-2 | 5 | 2.1-30.6% | haemoptysis; clinically fair |

Nine R10 cases have a tier-3 truth (pericarditis 4, laryngitis 2, anaemia, panic attack, viral pharyngitis 1 each); a NO on them is false reassurance. The hallmarks are DDXPlus likelihood ratios, so "PSVT is plausible because the patient has palpitations" is the generator's co-occurrence, not a clinical claim; the red-herring doc says so (section 6, limit 1), and the spec should carry the per-target cell rate and token so readers can see it. Undetermined cells matter little here: 2 R10 targets.

### 2.4 Circularity with the DXA reference row

R10's DXA-derived targets are, by definition, the pairs the DXA reader flags, so its MT DXA is 0 and its COV on those targets is 100. The reader at 10% still has H 19.2: 45 cases where the tier-1 truth has DXA p under 10% and nothing else is a target (pulmonary oedema 7, anaphylaxis 6, asthma 6, pneumonia 5, MI 5). In 32 of the 50 low-p tier-1 truths DXA ranks the truth first at 5-10% in a flat differential. So the section 8 row is not an informed ceiling; it is the key's own threshold read as a decision. The reader at 5% (H 0.4, COV 98.5, OC 0) is the key itself and should not appear as a comparison row.

## 3. Flag leniency

`v03_review_flags.py`; `flags_suspect_map_rows.csv`, `flags_earned_related.csv`, `flags_models250.csv`.

1. **The map's related rows are a different-diagnosis list, not a coding tolerance.** Tier-1 conditions have 8-50 related rows each. 116 lenient rows for tier-1 conditions are symptom codes or are owned by a lower-tier DDXPlus condition: GERD K21.9 is related to Boerhaave, laryngospasm, pancreatic neoplasm, MI, stable and unstable angina; pericarditis I30 to myocarditis, MI, PE, pulmonary oedema and both anginas; influenza, TB, bronchitis, bronchiectasis and COPD codes to pneumonia; panic attack F41.0 to PSVT. 570 map rows (codes repeated across conditions) match two or more tier-1 conditions leniently, 130 match six or more; I21 and I26 each match eight.
2. **On the stand-ins the lenient rule erases the measure.** COV: lenient 87-92, broader-only 47-67, lenient minus symptom codes and lower-tier-owned codes 68-88, strict 41-66. MT truth: lenient 0.0 for 18 of 19 models, strict 6-38. The codes that earned a match when no strict code did: AF I48.91 for PSVT (133 model x case), heart failure I50.9 for myocarditis (112), unstable angina I20.0 for stable angina (110), pneumonia J18.9 for asthma (73) and for pulmonary neoplasm (69), PE I26.99 for myocarditis (51) and asthma (45), panic attack F41.0 for PSVT (51), GERD K21.9 for MI (21). Most credit a different and often less dangerous diagnosis.
3. **Minimal restriction.** Count equivalent, narrower and broader. Broader keeps the three policy calls the v0.2 review named (I49.9 or R00.0 for PSVT, I20.9 for unstable angina; I50.9 is "related" for pulmonary oedema, so add it as a broader row if the call is to credit it). Report "related" as the lenient sensitivity row. The broader-only rule is within 1-6 COV points of strict on every stand-in, so the choice between them matters little; the choice between either and "related" is the whole measure.

## 4. Headline mechanics

`v03_review_models.py` (`models250.csv`), `v03_review_extra.py`.

### 4.1 Unreadable output as false reassurance

On the 250 set (R10 denominator 122) the stand-ins have 0-3 unreadable cases inside the denominator, except Gemini 3 Pro with 25: its H is 22.1, and 1.6 on readable cases. The rule is right for a safety benchmark and was pre-registered in v0.2, but it means a 3% format-failure rate is worth more H than any careful model's real misses (the other 18 rows: 0.0-9.0). Print H on readable cases and the unreadable share beside the headline, and keep the one retry.

### 4.2 Denominator, interval width, separation

The denominator is 227 or 234 (section 2.1). H events come almost entirely from tier-1 truths: across the 19 stand-ins, 92 of 100 H events are on cases whose truth is tier 1 and 8 on DXA-derived-only cases (23 such cases on the 250 set, 10 of them atrial fibrillation with PSVT as the target; no model says NO on more than 26% of them). So the DXA-derived targets widen COV's denominator but barely move H. Simulated 95% widths on the 470 sample: 3.7 points at H 2% (5.2 clustered in three conditions), 5.4 at 5% (12.2 clustered), 7.2 at 10% (20.0 clustered). Stand-ins: H 0.0-9.0 for 18 rows, 33 of 171 pairs separated by a paired condition bootstrap, 1 of 18 adjacent pairs. 118 cases (95 tier-2 truths, 23 tier-3 with a red flag or an R5-only target) enter neither the H nor the OC denominator.

### 4.3 YES with only non-dangerous flags

H does not fire, CON does not fire, COV is 0 for the case and MT counts a miss. On the stand-ins, 17-36% of YES answers carry no tier-1 flag under broader-only matching (Haiku 4.5 36, Sonnet 4.6 34, GPT-5.4 Mini 33, DeepSeek R1 30). H' (NO, unreadable, or YES with no tier-1 flag) moves Haiku from 0.8 to 16.4, Sonnet from 3.3 to 13.1, GPT-5.4 Mini from 3.3 to 12.3, and the top rows by 3-5 points. The spec's principle is that the wrong call is the only heavy penalty, and "YES, watch for sinusitis" is a wrong call by content if not by field. We recommend H' as a pre-registered sensitivity row and CON extended, not H' as the headline, because the v0.1 lists were differentials rather than flags.

### 4.4 The reference rows

Section 2.4 covers the DXA reader. The always-YES row needs its list defined (issue 6). The naive-Bayes row is the memorisation ceiling and is unaffected.

## 6. Tier rule

Denominators under the sensitivity rows (R10 cases, R10 targets): severity only 187, 231; Newman-Toker 223, 273; v0.3 234, 284; 1-source 265, 344. The asthma upgrade adds 11 R10 cases (10 truths, 1 DXA-derived) and 29 R5-only DXA targets, mostly in COPD and bronchitis patients; the count-floor row removes it. The rule is upgrade-only and citable, and its weakness is stated in `docs/tier-upgrade-sources.md`: the sources are frequency lists, so they cannot reach conditions rare in US, UK or Japanese practice. Two consequences the spec should state: stable angina is tier 1 by DDXPlus severity alone, so a NO on a stable angina patient is a headline event (18 R10 targets); and COPD, the truth behind 310 of 439 asthma high-risk candidates, is tier 2, so the same patient is a target when DXA says asthma and not when the model names COPD (a related code, which issue 3 stops crediting). Missing counterexamples (AF, COPD, TB, pericarditis) are listed in the spec's limits; AF matters most, because AF patients supply 10 of the 23 DXA-derived-only R10 cases on the 250 set through PSVT.

## 7. Other items before a paid run

1. **Prompt.** The v5 prompt in `inference/prompt.py` still defines ESCALATE_NOW and carries the 7:1 line; no v6 exists. The YES definition is a low bar and, with the verdict written first, will raise the YES rate; issue 1's tolerance is the guard, and a 50-case pilot on two models would measure it (spend).
2. **Parse rules.** `flags` has no rules and no parser (issue 9). `p_serious` is descriptive-missing only and enters nothing, so the field could be dropped or given a use (a Brier row against "has an R10 target" would be the v0.2 C measure under the new key).
3. **Reuse of v0.2 code.** `parse_v02`, `ConditionMap.resolve`, the condition table and the cluster bootstrap carry over; `CaseKey`, `case_outcomes`, `score_row` and `matches` do not. `v03_review_common.py` builds the key from the committed cells and hallmarks and scores every measure; it can seed the scorer, with tests on the reference rows.
4. **Sample IDs.** `eval-v02-adult.json` stores `serious` and `p_serious_risk` under the v0.2 key; the v0.3 key fields (targets, red-herring labels, clearly-low-risk) should live in a committed CSV keyed by case_id, hash-pinned, so the run and the scorer read the same key.
