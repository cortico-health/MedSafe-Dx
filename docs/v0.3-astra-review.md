Reviewed spec/v0.3-scoring.md draft 2 (commit dd0846b). Verbatim output below.

**Verdict: not yet fit for identifying rigorously established unsafe clinical choices. High confidence.** It is useful as a reproducible synthetic stress test, but its headline mixes confirmed synthetic disease, uncertain DXA-derived concerns, and insufficiently established safety labels. Those distinctions matter when calling individual responses “true failures.”

I verified the key’s hash and the 234/118/118 partition, reproduced reference scores, and checked reference classes against validation-split adults. Everything ran locally, read-only, without network calls. I do not reopen the fixed key-count, parser, or related-code issues.

**Weaknesses, ranked by severity**

1. **Critical — Risk targets conflate disease, possibility, and actionable concern.**  
   A tier-1 truth establishes the synthetic diagnosis, but does not independently establish that reassurance was unreasonable from the available intake. Conversely, coarse-class DXA associations become mandatory concerns even when fuller dataset evidence identifies another disease. Of 284 R10 targets, 84 are DXA-derived; 23 of those have class rates below 5%. The truth-identifying reference achieves **99.36% top-1 accuracy yet costs 50.64**, versus blanket concern’s 25.11. This demonstrates different objectives, not necessarily unsafe behavior by the reference.

   **Minimal fix:** separate truth-anchored misses from DXA-supported policy disagreements. Preserve all upgrade evidence, but reserve definitive “unsafe choice” claims for explicitly qualified evidence strata. A deterministic label is not automatically a validated construct.

2. **Critical — Red-herring removal indirectly creates penalties, violating the intended asymmetry.**  
   [The detector document](/home/claude/MedSafe-Dx/docs/dxa-red-herrings.md) says removal must never create an over-flag penalty because “never true in DDXPlus” need not mean clinically implausible. Yet **66/118 clearly-low-risk cases have removed DXA concerns at ≥5%; 47 have them at ≥10%**. Removal makes these cases eligible for OC. Example: `ddxplus_99692`, sarcoidosis, has DXA MI **55.07%**, removed using a **1/418** class rate. YES then costs one.

   Moreover, low-risk includes ten SLE and three anemia cases despite recorded external harm concerns.

   **Minimal fix:** distinguish “unsupported target” from “positive evidence of safety.” Preserve disputed concerns as OC exemptions; require affirmative, independently supported low-risk criteria before charging concern. Report the resulting smaller evaluable denominator.

3. **High — Hallmark classes and thresholds do not establish a reliable probability floor.**  
   Counts equate different symptoms and correlated values; bands pool materially different probabilities. MI hallmarks emphasize particular pain descriptions and radiation sites. Their apparent specificity can reflect generator structure.

   The rule is also **non-monotone**: stable-angina cases in the same one-hallmark, 10–20% class have rate **1.2563%**; DXA **10.97% survives**, while **15.71% is removed**. Increasing DXA support can remove a target.

   The 1% floor admits 57 DXA targets with 1–2% class rates; it is a convention, not a universal clinical threshold. Undetermined targets include **0/18** and **0/24** classes. Neither affects main SC because both patients already have tier-1 truths, but both affect coverage. Even 0/30 has a one-sided 95% upper bound of **9.5%**.

   **Minimal fix:** use interval-based supported/unsupported/uncertain classes, a monotone calibrated rule, and independent replication. Keep uncertain evidence as exemptions or exploratory targets, without full-strength failure claims. Condition-specific testing thresholds cannot justify one universal floor.

4. **High — The headline discourages blanket concern but does not solve gaming.**  
   Always YES costs **25.11**; the specified DXA reader costs **67.02**. At OC=30%, beating blanket concern requires H<**5.04%**. These are legitimate consequences of the chosen loss, but “the cost fixes gaming” overstates them: flags and diagnostic competence remain irrelevant to SC.

   The **118 free cases** permit either answer without consequence. Both supplementary pools contain only R10 cases—**150/150 and 100/100**—so blanket YES achieves **SC=0** there.

   The 12.5% Bayes argument also concerns *probability of key membership*, not disease probability. Once a DXA-derived target is known to exist, membership is certain; changing R10 to R12.5 does not resolve that conceptual mismatch.

   **Minimal fix:** retain continuous 7:1 costs, but call SC policy loss, show target-specific omissions alongside it, and pair enriched pools with matched low-risk controls. Distinguish reference diagnostic accuracy from suitability of its decision rule.

5. **High — Tier provenance is asymmetric without being uniformly strong.**  
   External frequency lists combine disease prevalence, reporting, detection, and harm. Two mentions do not estimate harm conditional on missing a diagnosis. Asthma’s upgrade rests on **one chart and two lawsuits**, while AF remains tier 2. Most baseline tier-1 assignments retain undocumented DDXPlus severity.

   There is also a rule exception: pancreatic neoplasm inherits **“Other Cancers,”** while broader categories such as cardiac dysrhythmia are rejected. This creates unequal evidentiary standards. Severity-only versus one-source variants change R10 case counts from **187 to 265**.

   **Minimal fix:** retain upgrade-only tiers, but attach evidence-strength strata, formalize category exceptions, and restore the asthma count-floor sensitivity. Avoid presenting every tier-1 assignment as equally externally validated. [Provenance](/home/claude/MedSafe-Dx/docs/tier-upgrade-sources.md)

6. **High — Intervals omit key uncertainty and power is inadequate for fine rankings.**  
   With frozen hallmarks and **108,372 validation adults**, I found **15 target-status changes**. R10 remains 234, but `ddxplus_116571` loses its sole myocarditis target, while `ddxplus_51583` gains dystonic reactions. Aggregate agreement hides individual label instability.

   The bootstrap resamples the 47 truth conditions, changing their mix despite fixed condition-stratified sampling. It does not propagate hallmark, class-rate, tier, or repeated-generation uncertainty. It also returns **H=[0,0]** when no misses occur. Even under independent cases, zero misses among 234 only gives a one-sided 95% upper bound of **1.27%**. Ten cases detect a condition-specific 5% failure rate with only **40% probability**.

   **Minimal fix:** define the sampling estimand; use within-condition resampling for the fixed condition mix, retain condition resampling for a stated superpopulation sensitivity, add boundary-valid bounds and separate key-uncertainty analyses. Increase cases based on detectable failure rates, not desired leaderboard size.

7. **High — Prevalence, closed-world structure, and memorisation constrain interpretation.**  
   R10 prevalence is **49.8%**, falling to about **36%** under the repository’s ED weighting. Reweighting cannot restore absent diseases, comorbidity, or age-dependent presentations. The repository reports **2–15-fold real-world age gradients** that DDXPlus lacks. Its synthetic exclusivity also suppresses legitimate competing diagnoses.

   Near-perfect naive Bayes shows that high accuracy can arise from generator shortcuts; it does **not** establish that an LLM memorised test cases.

   **Minimal fix:** report disease-conditioned results and mix-specific baselines; audit exact/near duplicates and performance without antecedent shortcuts; test meaning-preserving rendering variants. State that split independence does not establish independence from model training.

8. **High — Calibration against ideal, noise, DXA, and humans remains incomplete.**  
   Zero is an algebraic oracle score, not an attainable clinical optimum. No validated label-noise floor exists. DXA is partly embedded in the targets, so agreement is not independent validation. Published human figures—such as **1.5% MI diagnostic error** or **97% safe urgency advice**—measure different tasks, information, populations, and outcomes.

   **Minimal fix:** print four explicit anchors: oracle loss, label-stability range, actual DXA-policy loss, and **human comparison unavailable**. Literature rates can provide contextual comparisons, but cannot calibrate SC into clinician-equivalent error rates. [Human-source discussion](/home/claude/MedSafe-Dx/docs/dangerous-if-missed-labels.md)

9. **Medium — Prompt effects can dominate the measured behavior.**  
   “Potentially serious,” exhaustive symptom absence, and verdict-first generation jointly define a particular decision task. The earlier models’ **74–88% YES rates** came from another prompt; their ranking stability cannot validate v6. Exhaustive negatives also amplify generator shortcuts.

   **Minimal fix:** pre-register a small paired prompt/order/negative-information ablation before the full run. Report each model–prompt configuration separately; freeze choices without selecting whichever produces the preferred rankings.

10. **Medium — Secondary-score defects weaken the diagnostic safeguards.**  
    In [the current scorer](/home/claude/MedSafe-Dx/evaluator/v03_score.py), an empty differential receives **D1 Brier=0**, because both forecast and correctness become zero. D2 misses confident MI→PE errors because both are tier 1, and MI→GERD because the tier gap is only one.

    Reference reporting also disagrees: current DXA COV is **62.17%**, whereas the headline-design table reports **96.1%**.

    **Minimal fix:** exclude missing forecasts from calibration with completeness reported; add all confident-wrong diagnoses separately from tier-gap errors; regenerate reference tables through the production scorer.

**What earlier reviews missed:** the indirect OC penalty from red-herring removal, non-monotone inclusion, unchanged totals hiding validation-split label swaps, perfect blanket-concern pool scores, degenerate zero-event intervals, and empty-differential calibration credit.

**Must fix before full run:** evidence-status separation; OC eligibility; independent key validation and uncertainty handling; scorer/reference discrepancies; sampling and power specification; prompt ablation registration.

**State as limits:** synthetic prevalence, closed-world exclusions, possible training contamination, incomplete tier evidence, provisional 7:1 preferences, and absence of comparable human or clinical label-noise calibration. These limits require narrower claims even after implementation fixes.
